## Design decisions

- **Pages are named as OpenStax names them** (its Word files and web addresses), checked against the Word files of the corpus's six books: 1,379 of 1,386 names match, and the other 7 are the answer key pages of *Principles of Data Science*, empty here without its exercises. The rules: section `3-1-…` from its numbered title; introduction `{n}-{title}`; end-of-chapter page `{n}-{title}`; answer key page `chapter-{n}`; appendix `{letter}-{title}`; a unit's own page `{unit}-{title}`. Slugs: lowercase, apostrophes dropped, accents stripped, `®` as `r`, everything else a hyphen.
- **Notes are `div`s**, titled by heading. `<aside>` gains nothing: Pandoc reads it as a `Div` with class `aside`, and writes that back.
- **A page's title is its first `h1`**, the introduction's included (OpenStax puts it after the opening figure and outline); **a table's caption is a `<caption>`** at its top; **footnotes are Pandoc's** (`role="doc-noteref"`, a `role="doc-endnotes"` section), numbered through each chapter, as OpenStax numbers them, by `notes.numbering: group` in `conversion.yaml`, each page keeping its own at its end; a unit is `type: part` in `project.yaml`, so each of its chapters numbers on its own, and a page at the top level, a preface or an appendix, is a group of its own (`main`'s `notes.groups`, 1192f59). Measured, HTML and EPUB, on *Entrepreneurship* (its 15 chapters with notes, chapter 1's four pages with them numbered 1 to 4, 5 to 20, and 21 to 24) and *Concepts of Biology* (11 chapters with notes in six units, Chapter 7 and Chapter 8 of Unit 2 each from 1): every chapter numbered from 1 through its pages, and the EPUB's numbers the pages'.

## Traps for whoever ports the next recipe

Cookbook writes its markup in templates whose line breaks fall between elements: `<span class="os-title-label">Think It Through</span>`, a line break, `<span class="os-number">1.1</span>`. That line break is text, and shows as a space ("Think It Through 1.1"); a port that appends the two spans shows "Think It Through1.1". The samples can't catch it: cookbook's `scripts/normalize` reads the expected output with `noblanks`, dropping every space between elements, before its tests compare, so `cnx-compare-cookbook.py` counts each element's edge as a break and can't tell. Found so in BakeNumberedNotes.v1's heading and BakeExercisePrefixes's prefix ("TP 1.", as openstax.org shows it); fixed. When porting, look at each template for elements on lines of their own.

A word the locale doesn't have is written out as such: a note of a kind its strings don't name is titled "unknown title for note with classes [...]", and a string missing from a Polish recipe's `pl.yml` would read "translation missing" (cookbook's I18n has no fallback to English). Look at a recipe's note classes against its locale.

And a search with two selectors, Kitchen's `search(a, b)`, returns what the first finds and then what the second does, not the two in the order they stand: *World History* numbers a page's own exercises and then its bank questions, wherever each stands. A port that takes `a, b` as one XPath union gets document order and numbers them differently; the samples catch this one.
