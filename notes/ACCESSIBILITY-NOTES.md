# Accessibility notes

What the accessibility standards say (WCAG, PDF/UA, WTPDF), what screen readers say of the pages, PDFs, Word files, and decks TextbookImprover makes, and what the checkers report and miss (veraPDF, axe-core, Ace and epubcheck, the Nu HTML checker, and others). Most of what a screen reader said here was heard with NVDA on Windows, in a browser, with Acrobat, in Word, and in PowerPoint; other screen readers have a few entries each, and results with them are what the file most needs. Each result names who heard it and when, with the versions where they were recorded, and says what was heard, not what should have been. What LaTeX's tagging code writes into a PDF is in [LATEX-NOTES.md](LATEX-NOTES.md); the decisions that followed from these tests are in [DESIGN-NOTES.md](DESIGN-NOTES.md). [README.md](README.md) describes how an entry is written.

## What the standards say

### WCAG

**`lib/wcag.py`'s success criteria are W3C's own.** They're taken from w3c/wcag at 457cece (2026-10-19): the number from `guidelines/index.html`'s order, the handle and level from each criterion's file, and the version from its folder (20, 21, 22). That's 87 criteria, 61 from 2.0, 17 from 2.1, and 9 from 2.2, with 4.1.1 without a level. Every Understanding page is in the repository's gh-pages branch. The 65 WCAG standards the findings already named all agreed with it, and a unit test holds them to it.

**Text used as a symbol isn't a visible label to WCAG 2.5.3, Label in Name** (W3C's Understanding document, read 2026-10-13). The output check takes that as text with no letter or digit, so a symbol made of letters (an "X" to close) is still checked for a label without its text.

**WCAG 1.4.10, Reflow, lets a data table scroll, and a layout table isn't among its exceptions** (read 2026-10-19). So `figures-and-tables.lua` wraps a layout table in no focusable scroll region, and `page.css` stacks its cells on a narrow screen.

**WCAG 1.4.8 (AAA) doesn't say what its space-and-a-half is measured from.** W3C's technique C21, one of the criterion's sufficient techniques, which W3C says meet it, measures space-and-a-half from the type's size (CSS's `line-height: 150%`), where W3C's explanation of the criterion measures it from single spacing, "the default spacing for the font", 1.2 times the type's size in LaTeX; only the criterion's own words are normative, and they don't say (read 2026-10-21). The PDF target's page for WCAG 1.4.8 counts spacing from the type's size.

### Other guidance

**What published guidance says of type size and line length** (read 2026-10-19). Stanford's university IT gives 12 points as the minimum for Word and PDF documents, and says 12-point text with standard margins gives a line of suitable length (50 to 120 characters). The EU Publications Office's checklist for an accessible Word file gives 12 points for the body, 9 for footnotes, and left alignment, not justified. RNIB says regular print is usually 10 or 12 points and clear print 14. WCAG 1.4.8 (AAA) asks for a way to get lines of 80 characters or fewer, not justified, 1.5 apart.

**ADA Title II's compliance date moved, and its standard didn't.** A Department of Justice interim final rule of April 20, 2026 extended the ADA Title II compliance date for entities serving 50,000 or more (effectively every public university) to April 26, 2027, leaving WCAG 2.1 AA as the standard. This comes from research done by 2026-10-03 and wasn't verified against the rule itself; not checked since. The rule is interim, so it's to be checked again before anyone cites it.

### PDF/UA and Well-Tagged PDF

**A PDF/UA-2 file LaTeX makes claims Well-Tagged PDF, and veraPDF checks what a file claims.** Read 2026-10-03 in the PDF Association's Well-Tagged PDF 1.0.0 (February 2024): a PDF/UA-2 file LaTeX makes carries `pdfd:conformsTo` declarations for `http://pdfa.org/declarations/wtpdf#accessibility1.0` and `#reuse1.0` (read from the PDF target's XMP), and veraPDF applies what a file claims, so both WTPDF 1.0 profiles run beside PDF/UA-2 and Tagged PDF (`pdfcheck.run_verapdf` says so, measured on veraPDF 1.30). The irregular-table findings a book's row spans first gave came from them (rule 8.2.5.26-4, in the ISO 32005 and both WTPDF profiles). The specification writes the identifiers with a slash before `#` (`wtpdf/#accessibility1.0`); veraPDF accepts LaTeX's form. A PDF/UA-1 file is PDF 1.7 and outside WTPDF.

**WTPDF's points that bear on the PDF target's output** (WTPDF 1.0.0, read 2026-10-03): tables shall be regular, and row groups regular with no cell spanning one (8.2.5.26); `Title` is never a heading (8.2.5.13); WTPDF doesn't require sequential heading levels, unlike PDF/UA-1 (8.2.5.12, note 2); a Formula needs Alt or ActualText only for non-mathematical formulae, math being MathML structure or a MathML associated file with `AFRelationship` Supplement (8.2.5.29); and `DisplayDocTitle` true and `dc:title` are required (8.11).

**Deriving HTML from PDF maps a Well-Tagged PDF's structure tree to HTML and CSS, element by element**, and warns that a file not conforming to WTPDF gives unpredictable results (the PDF Association's Deriving HTML from PDF 1.2, September 2026, read 2026-10-03). The roadmap's "PDF as input, later" has three tiers by it: a file claiming WTPDF derived to HTML by the specification and read by the HTML reader; a tagged file not claiming it, the same with its problems reported; and an untagged one, its text layer as Markdown with guessed structure, or bare text. The PDF target's own PDFs would be the first round-trip test.

**The PDF Association's technical notes for PDF/UA-1 say a Link inside a Reference is correct, and that an image of text is a Figure with ActualText.** TN-PDFUA1-001 v1.4.1 (ActualText on Figure) and TN-PDFUA1-002 v1.4 (Reference containing Link) were read on 2026-10-03 and measured against a two-chapter book built as PDF/UA-2 and PDF/UA-1 (a contents page, a footnote, an external link, and two `\includegraphics[actualtext=...]` images of text, put in as raw LaTeX). LaTeX's contents page is TOC, TOCI (with Ref), Reference, Link, and the link annotation, which is TN-002's interactive table-of-contents example exactly, in both standards and whether the contents page is LaTeX's default or placed by `generate: toc` in `project.contents` (the PDF build writes LaTeX's own there). TN-002 says nesting Link inside Reference is correct and a viewer that makes a screen reader say "link" twice has the defect; WTPDF 8.2.5.20 says Reference *should* be used for links within the document, and LaTeX tags the text's own internal links as Link. The `actualtext` key gives a Span with ActualText inside the P, in both standards; that's WTPDF's own example (8.2.2, example 8), but for PDF/UA-1 TN-001 says an image that is text should be a Figure with ActualText and Placement Inline inside a block, since PDF/UA-1 7.3 has every non-text graphic tagged Figure. veraPDF's PDF/UA-1 profile accepts LaTeX's Span (nothing found). As of 2026-10-03 the pipeline gives no image ActualText: every image is a Figure with alt text, or an artifact. A possible addition, on the roadmap (Images of text): a marker in `image-alt.csv` for an image that is text (a word-art heading, a decorated initial), giving `actualtext` in the PDF and the same text as alt in HTML, EPUB, and Word, with the PDF/UA-1 form an open question for the LaTeX team. Both notes attach functional PDF/UA-1 example files, which would be fixtures for reading PDF one day. For testing with a screen reader, both notes say a viewer that announces a figure for text with ActualText, or a link twice, has the defect, to be reported to its publisher.

**ISO 32000-2 lets a structure element be empty** (14.8.4.2, in a note: "Structure elements can be empty"; read 2026-10-24). LaTeX's tagging makes a matrix's empty row an `mtd` structure element with nothing in it, as the source's MathML has it, and a formula's spaces empty `mspace` elements: built as a PDF, a page of two evaluation bars has six `mtd` elements, two of them empty, and 16 empty `mspace` elements, and veraPDF's PDF/UA-2 profile passes the PDF.

**PDF 1.7 and PDF 2.0 both say a `Formula` should have `/Alt` or `/ActualText`.** PDF 1.7 lists `Formula` among its illustration elements, like `Figure`, which should have `/Alt` or `/ActualText` (ISO 32000-1, 14.8.4.5), and PDF 2.0 says the same of `Formula` (ISO 32000-2, 14.8.4.8.6) (read 2026-10-10). A deck's PDF from ltx-talk whose formulas carried their MathML only as associated files had neither. What NVDA does with a formula's `/Alt` is under NVDA with Acrobat, Formulas.

## Alt text, long descriptions, and link text

### Alt text and long descriptions

**WCAG has no character limit for alt text, at any level.** The 120 or 125 figure often quoted traces to [Terrill Thompson's test page](https://terrillthompson.com/tests/altlength.html), which found that JAWS 6.0 "reads ALT text in 125 character chunks ... it divides it into multiple graphics". It divides, and doesn't truncate: nothing is lost, and a user steps through it. [Eric Eggert's article](https://yatil.net/blog/there-is-no-character-limit-for-alt-text) confirms no screen reader cuts alt text off, and puts the myth down to people seeing the split and assuming loss. JAWS 6.0 is from about 2005. There's an [open W3C discussion](https://github.com/w3c/wcag/discussions/4047) on the whole question (read by 2026-10-03).

**Tim Prescott's guidance for alt text is at most 120 characters or two sentences, objective, and not starting with "image"** ([Accessible PDF files using LaTeX](https://teepeemm.github.io/accessibility/), read 2026-10-03). That matches `images.alt_max_chars` (120); the "image" opening isn't checked (as of 2026-10-03).

**W3C gives no length at which an image needs a long description.** [The Complex Images tutorial](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/complex/) asks for a two-part alternative, a short description plus a long one, for graphs, charts, diagrams the text depends on, and maps. Clemson's guidance attributes a 500-character threshold to WAI; the W3C page doesn't have it (read by 2026-10-03), so 500 isn't to be cited to W3C.

**What decides where a long description goes is its structure, not its length.** W3C says `aria-describedby` "is treated as one continuous paragraph of text" with no access to headings or tables, so it "only works for long descriptions that are text-only". A description needing a table needs `figure`/`figcaption`. W3C also says long descriptions "benefit many people, and it is good practice to make them available to everyone, for example, as part of the main content", which for a textbook usually means the prose, not an attribute (read by 2026-10-03).

**A tagged PDF keeps both a figure's caption and its image's alt text, even where they're the same words.** Pandoc's Markdown reader gives a figure's image its caption for alt text when it has none, and a tagged PDF has both, a Caption and the Figure's /Alt, so NVDA reads the words twice (NVDA with Acrobat, Text). Pandoc's HTML writer hides such a caption (`aria-hidden`; PANDOC-NOTES, The HTML writer), and a deck's PowerPoint file marks it decorative, but PDF/UA-2 requires caption text in a Caption (ISO 14289-2, 8.2.5.27) and a Figure's alternate description or replacement text (8.2.5.28.2), so the PDF is left as it is (2026-11-03). The audit reports a figure's image whose alt text is its caption (`docs/auditing.md`).

**Office's newer generated descriptions end "AI-generated content may be incorrect."** Clemson's guide quotes it, and three pictures in a course's decks had it (2026-10-10). `AUTO_GENERATED` in `lib/alttext.py` takes it.

### Bare URLs

**A bare URL in a reference list doesn't fail WCAG 2.4.4.** 2.4.4 (Level A) allows the purpose to come from context, the enclosing sentence, paragraph, or list item, and a citation is that context. 2.4.9 (AAA) requires the link text alone, which a bare URL fails; almost nobody targets AAA. One source states the carve-out explicitly: link descriptive words "unless the document's purpose is specifically to display the URL itself (for example, in citation formats)". So a reference list's bare URLs conform, and are still a bad experience for a listener (NVDA in a browser, Links) (read by 2026-10-03).

**WCAG 2.5.3, Label in Name, rules out an `aria-label` that describes a bare URL.** An `aria-label` on a link replaces its accessible name, and 2.5.3 (Level A, added in 2.1) requires the name to contain the visible text. "DOI for Klein and Stern 2005" doesn't contain the URL it labels, so each labeled bare URL fails a Level A criterion to gain a AAA one (2.4.9), and a reference list that conforms at AA with bare URLs stops conforming with the labels. A manual audit would log it; the automated checkers wouldn't notice. The conforming design keeps the name as the visible text and puts the description in the accessible description (a `title`, or `aria-describedby`), and in PDF writes `/Contents` as the visible text followed by the description. W3C's PDF13 technique replaces a link's text with `/Alt` and uses a bare URL as its example, but it predates 2.5.3 and is listed as sufficient only for 2.4.4 and 2.4.9, so it isn't a defense (read by 2026-10-03). The same research found every unlabeled link in the sample PDFs it looked at carrying `tagpdf`'s literal `url` as `/Contents`, which Acrobat would announce in place of the link text; that was never confirmed in Acrobat with NVDA, and the PDF target, new in 0.8 (2026-10-03), writes each of the text's links its `/Contents` itself, its visible text and then its description (`docs/formats.md`).

## NVDA, in any program

**NVDA says a column's header only when the column changes, and a row's when the row changes** ([speech.py](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/source/speech/speech.py#L2095-L2132), release 2026.2, read 2026-10-10): moving down a column never repeats its header, in any table.

**NVDA says "not supported" when its synthesizer has no voice for a language it detects** (2026-10-11). So hearing it of a passage in a PDF means its `/Lang` was found. A voice is added in Windows (Settings, Time & language, Speech, Add voices), with NVDA restarted after, or eSpeak NG has one.

**NVDA reads U+0302 "hat" from 2025.2 and "normalizes" from 2022.1 to 2025.1, and normalizes speech with NFKC by default since 2025.1** (by NVDA's release, looked up 2026-10-23). On samples of a letter typed in text with a combining hat or bar, NVDA read correctly only the two forms wanted for math, and in words typed with a combining circumflex it said "hat" rather than reading the obvious English words (Rob, 2026-10-24; the samples weren't kept). So `math.from_text` takes a letter with a hat or a bar as a variable where it stands alone, and a letter with a hat in a word stays the word's (`docs/configuration.md`, Math).

## NVDA in a browser

### Tables

**NVDA handles a banded table fully only when it's split into a table per band.** Tested with NVDA in Chrome and Firefox on a test page of the four forms of a banded table, moving across rows and down columns (Rob, before 0.8, 2026-10-03; no date or NVDA version was recorded). NVDA announces only the header of the dimension that changed, the row header moving down and the column header moving across. A split table's caption is read on arrival. Of the three grouped HTML forms, a band as a spanning `<th scope="rowgroup">` row, with or without explicit `headers` ids, is named only on the band's own cell; the group as a column of its own, `rowspan` over its rows (W3C's pattern for irregular headers), is named with the row header on every data cell moving down, though whether the column header came too was inconsistent. So `tables.bands` is `split` for HTML, EPUB, and PDF (`docs/sidecars.md`).

**A table region's name with math in its caption is read twice, with the math read differently each time, whatever names the region.** The pipeline names a wide table's scrolling wrapper with its caption flattened to text, so math arrives as TeX (`Selected values for Q=100 \sqrt{LK}`, from *Principles of Economics*). Tested with NVDA 2026.2 (2026.2.0.57664; MathCAT is built in since 2026.1) in Chrome, on a page of two of the book's tables in three versions each (Rob, 2026-09-25): the label as written now, `aria-labelledby` pointing to the caption, and a label from Pandoc's plain-text rendering (Unicode `MC = ΔTC/ΔQ`, but TeX again for a square root). All three are poor in the same way: the caption is read twice, as the region's name and as the caption, with the math read differently each time. So the label stays as it is. MathSpeak text for it, if it can be made reliably, is on the roadmap; better screen-reader support for math may make it unnecessary.

**A column whose header cell holds only a formula has no header for NVDA to announce, in a browser as in Acrobat.** NVDA's browser support renders no children of a `math` element and puts a space in their place ([gecko_ia2.cpp](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/nvdaHelper/vbufBackends/gecko_ia2/gecko_ia2.cpp#L849-L856), [L1322–L1327](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/nvdaHelper/vbufBackends/gecko_ia2/gecko_ia2.cpp#L1322-L1327)), and takes a header's text the same way, as its Acrobat support does (release 2026.2, read 2026-10-10). So an HTML or EPUB table whose column header is a formula alone has the same gap as a PDF's, and math books have many (x and f(x) heading a table of values). Heard on the `html` target's page of a table headed Price, q_d alone, and "Revenue, p·q_d": NVDA read the formulas as math, in the paragraph and in the header row, and moving into a column it announced a header's words and nothing of its formula (Rob, 2026-10-10; the PDFs of the same page are under NVDA with Acrobat, Formulas). The output check notes such a cell (`table-header-is-formula`, `docs/checking.md`).

### Links and landmarks

**NVDA reads a bare URL whole before any description conforming markup gives it.** Tested with NVDA in Chrome on a test page of seven variants (Rob, 2026-09-24): a `title`, `aria-describedby` to a hidden span, `aria-describedby` to the visible citation, both, an `aria-label` of the URL and the description, and an `aria-label` of the description alone. On Tab, NVDA+Tab, and reading line by line, every variant but the last reads the whole URL and then the description; only the replacing `aria-label`, which fails 2.5.3, spares the listener the URL. Hovering gives the URL. So no conforming description shortens what's heard, and a bare link's remedy is the bare-links sidecar's: a replacement URL (a shortDOI, which APA allows for long DOIs) or replacement text, with a `title` column for a tooltip and a Word ScreenTip.

**NVDA announces a section with a DPUB-ARIA role as a generic landmark, never by the role's own word.** DPUB-AAM maps the `doc-` roles to landmarks in every platform API, but NVDA's `aria.py` knows only the seven core landmarks and no `doc-` role, and DAISY's knowledge base calls support not yet widespread (read 2026-10-08). On a test page of lettered sections, NVDA says "region" after B, a `region` as the control, and "landmark" after C, D, and F, the named `doc-preface`, `doc-glossary`, and `doc-appendix`, and "landmark" before E, the unnamed `doc-glossary`: a generic landmark, the name its label gives it, and never the role's own word (glossary, preface). The same in Firefox and Chrome, and the elements list's Landmarks show B, C, D, and F by name under main and E as a blank line (Rob, 2026-10-09). So HTML pages don't get the roles (on a page that is wholly a glossary the landmark adds nothing its heading doesn't say), and the EPUB names each typed section by its heading (`name_section` in `build-epub.py`: `aria-labelledby`, the heading given an id when it has none, only when the heading comes first in the section).

### Text

**NVDA read an en dash written for a subtraction as a range, and said nothing for one written for a negative sign.** Heard on section 23.1 of _Principles of Economics 3e_ (Rob, 2026-11-06). So `math-repair.lua` makes an en dash in text a minus sign where it's a number's sign or a subtraction's (`math.minus_in_text`), which it did 2,166 times on ten of OpenStax's books.

### A deck's pages and its reveal.js presentation

**Pandoc's plain HTML page of a deck worked well with NVDA in Chrome, formulas and tables included** (Rob, 2026-10-09).

**A Markdown deck's pages read with their headings as expected** (Rob, 2026-10-11, NVDA in a browser, on the pages of four of the test suite's decks, before that day's fixes to a deck's headings): the notes' disclosure works, "A Cue" in the notes is an H5, and "Quoted big heading" is read as a block quote and a heading.

**A reveal.js presentation from the `revealjs` target is read as a page by NVDA in Chrome** (Rob, 2026-10-14; Firefox wasn't tried that day). As expected: the title slide read as a page; the footnote, its way back, and the link to a slide; formulas as math, the display equation too; steps read as they appear, the picture's alt text once, the blocks' headings; the long slide fitted, the one too long scrolling, and the page at 200%; and a conference deck's sections and slides in order. "I tried multiple ways of moving through slides and text and everything seemed to work reasonably well." Column headers were announced moving across a table, not down it, which is NVDA's own rule (NVDA, in any program). Of Spanish, NVDA recognized the language and had no voice for it.

**MathCAT says "bold" before each letter of a formula in `\mathbf`, and reads a name typed as TeX's letters letter by letter.** In sample deck S05's presentation, "Bob's Demand for Water" reads perfectly; "Market Demand" reads its headers as math, but says "bold" before each letter, and Alice's and Bob's names letter by letter (Rob, 2026-10-14, NVDA in Chrome). The headers are `\mathbf{q^{d}_{Alice}}` and the like: bold in a formula means something to MathCAT; texmath writes `\mathbf`'s letters bold italic (`mi mathvariant="bold-italic"`, 𝒒; PANDOC-NOTES), where LaTeX sets them upright; and `Alice` in TeX is five variables. `q^{d}_{\text{Alice}}`, if the bold is only for looks, should read as a name, without "bold", the header cell bold anyway on the page.

**reveal.js's progress bar was read as "space" after the controls, and the `revealjs` target hides it.** Arrowing down past the controls' landmark, NVDA read "space" (Rob, 2026-10-14, Chrome). In Chromium's accessibility tree, the only thing after the controls is reveal.js's progress bar, `div.progress` holding an empty `span`, a strip a mouse can click to go to a slide. Chrome gives an element that listens for clicks a click action, and NVDA's browser buffer shows an interactive element with nothing in it as a space, so a reader can reach it (`gecko_ia2.cpp`: any `IAccessibleAction` named `click` makes a node interactive, and an interactive node with no content is rendered as `EMPTY_TEXT_NODE`, a space; read 2026-10-14). With the progress bar hidden, there was no "space" after the controls, in Chrome and Firefox, and nothing was reported reading differently in Firefox (Rob, 2026-10-14).

## NVDA with Acrobat

### What NVDA's source does

**NVDA's Acrobat support puts a single space in its buffer for a `Formula` and doesn't look inside**: "We don't want the content of formulas, but we still want a space so the user can get at them" ([adobeAcrobat.cpp](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/nvdaHelper/vbufBackends/adobeAcrobat/adobeAcrobat.cpp#L456-L460), [the space](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/nvdaHelper/vbufBackends/adobeAcrobat/adobeAcrobat.cpp#L621-L627); release 2026.2, read 2026-10-10).

**A cell's header is the buffer's text for its header cell** ([virtualBuffers/\_\_init\_\_.py](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/source/virtualBuffers/__init__.py#L416-L437); release 2026.2, read 2026-10-10), so a header cell holding only a formula has a space for its header, whatever the formula carries: `/Alt`, `/ActualText`, and MathML alike.

**The math itself is read from the formula when the reader reaches it**, which is why a header row of formulas reads well: Office's `/MSFT_MathML` attribute first, then MathML structure elements, then the node's value, wrapped as `mtext` when it isn't MathML ([IAccessible/adobeAcrobat.py](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/source/NVDAObjects/IAccessible/adobeAcrobat.py#L189-L234); release 2026.2, read 2026-10-10). Acrobat evidently gives a formula's `/Alt` as that value when it has one, ahead of the attached MathML, which is what the PDFs whose formulas had alt text showed (Formulas, below); structure elements come before the value, so in NVDA they'd win over an `/Alt` (not tested).

**A header cell's own `/Alt` or `/ActualText` is read only when the cell has no children in Acrobat's accessibility tree** (`renderText` reads them, [L138–L145](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/nvdaHelper/vbufBackends/adobeAcrobat/adobeAcrobat.cpp#L138-L145), and is called only for a leaf, [L661–L694](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/blob/release-2026.2/nvdaHelper/vbufBackends/adobeAcrobat/adobeAcrobat.cpp#L661-L694); release 2026.2, read 2026-10-10), and a header cell that holds a formula has children.

**NVDA's Acrobat support counts only `H` and `H1` to `H6` as headings** (`normalizeStdName`, read 2026-10-11), and LaTeX maps `Title` to `P` for older readers, so a document's title is probably read as text. A deck's title slide's title was heard as no heading (A deck's PDF from ltx-talk, below).

### A test PDF from the PDF target

**NVDA with Acrobat Reader read every element of a short test PDF from the PDF target as intended, once a table's caption was retagged and a figure tagged where it stands** (Rob, 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-02). The PDF held two tables the same but for a caption, a matrix with row headers, an image and a decorative rule, links, math, and a footnote. As first built: the captioned table's caption was read as row 1 content, with "four rows" and "six columns" for a three-by-three table, and the uncaptioned one three and three. Links read as expected, the description included. Math read perfectly (MathML structure elements). The footnote read, its link and text following. The image wasn't heard: LaTeX had floated it, and latex-lab's float code gathers a floating figure's tags into a `figures` container at the end of the document (`float/defer`, `latex-lab-testphase-float.sty`), so it came after everything. Moving across a row announces column headers; moving down a column (Ctrl+Alt+Down) is what announces a row header. Retested that way (2026-10-02), the row header is read, and not the column header, so latex-lab's TH-row scope (given through the ClassMap) reaches NVDA. NVDA also says the cell's coordinates before its content ("Left column 2 2" in column 2, row 3), which is its own default, not the PDF's: NVDA's Document Formatting settings turn cell coordinates off. With the two repairs below, every element read as intended: captions, column and row headers, figures in place, a decorative image skipped, link descriptions, MathML, and footnotes.

**With a figure tagged where it stands, NVDA reads its caption and then its image** (`pdf.figures: in_place`, the default): "Caption figure 1. A small red square. Out of caption. Graphic a small red square." (Rob, 2026-10-01/02).

**With a table's caption retagged as a Caption, NVDA reads it as one and counts the table's rows and columns rightly** (`pdf.repair_captions`, `lib/pdfretag.py`): with a prototype of the repair on the test PDF, NVDA read "Caption. Table 1 Output and price by year. Out of caption." and three rows and three columns (Rob, 2026-10-01/02). The six columns had come from the empty repeated head LaTeX's tagging leaves in the `Table` (tagging-project#1583).

### Formulas

**NVDA reads a formula's `/Alt` in place of its MathML, and reads MathML as structure elements best.** Three PDFs of sample deck S05 were built with ltx-talk, its tables retagged and its frame titles H1, each with one change, all passing veraPDF's PDF/UA-2 profile (2026-10-10). One gives each formula the `/Alt` LaTeX writes itself with `\tagpdfsetup{math/alt/use=true}` ("LaTeX formula starts \begin {math} … \end {math} LaTeX formula ends"). One gives it words, made from its MathML by the speech rule engine's ClearSpeak style (npm's `speech-rule-engine` 4; "q sub Alice to the d-th power"), luamml's `mathvariant="normal"` on a bold letter taken off first, since the engine read it "normal q". ### The WCAG 2.2 machine profile and its modules

**veraPDF 1.30.3, as released, doesn't run its WCAG 2.2 machine profile's own checks.** The profile (`PDF_UA/WCAG-2-2-Machine.xml` and `-PDF20.xml` in veraPDF-validation-profiles) holds the rules PDF4WCAG reports, named as PDF4WCAG names them. Its own checks are on objects of the semantic layer (`SASpan`, `SARepeatedCharacters`, `SALinkAnnotation`), which the CLI jar declares (`org/verapdf/model/salayer`) but whose implementations, veraPDF-validation's `wcag-validation` and the `veraPDF-wcag-algs` library, it doesn't include, and `GFCosDocument.getdocument` adds the semantic document only when `GFSAPDFDocument` loads. Given the profile with `-p`, the release reported two PDF/UA-1 rules and passed the WCAG rules unchecked (measured 2026-10-19). Compiled from both repositories at the tag v1.30.3 against the CLI jar (javac, JDK 21; `ContrastRatioConsumer`, which needs PDFBox, replaced by a stand-in, so 1.4.3-1, contrast, wasn't checked) and put on the script's `CLASSPATH_PREFIX`, the release runs it: on _Instrumental Analysis_'s PDF, 60 s, four rules failed, 4.1.2-17 12 times, 1.3.1-2 11 times, 2.4.9-1 3 times, the three a validator's report had listed, and 1.3.1-1 once, "7 repeated non space characters": Table 25.7.1's row of groups, I, II, III, IV, the numerals of adjacent cells chained into one run (the algorithm chains text on one baseline whose edge characters match). That one is the table's content, and is left. Nothing needs compiling: the modules are on Maven Central (next).

**veraPDF publishes its WCAG modules on Maven Central: with what they need, nine jars, 5.5 MB** (2026-10-21/22). veraPDF publishes `wcag-validation` and `wcag-algorithms` there, and with what `wcag-algorithms` 1.30.3's POM names (PDFBox 3.0.4, which draws each page for the contrast check, `jai-imageio-core` 1.4.0, `jai-imageio-jpeg2000` 1.3.0, `jbig2-imageio` 3.0.3) and PDFBox's own (`pdfbox-io`, `fontbox`, `commons-logging` 1.3.4), that's nine jars, 5.5 MB. The launcher puts `CLASSPATH_PREFIX` ahead of its own `bin/*`, and a folder's `/*` loads every jar in it. The release's document model adds the semantic layer only when `Class.forName` loads `GFSAPDFDocument`, catching any Throwable (`GFCosDocument.getdocument`, v1.30.3), so a folder named without the `/*`, or one without `wcag-algorithms`, gives a silent pass (measured with the modules built from source, split into the two jars). ## axe-core

### Rules and tags

**axe-core 4.14.0 has 60, 63, and 64 rules at AA for WCAG 2.0, 2.1, and 2.2, 63, 66, and 67 at AAA, and 27 best-practice rules** (as built, 2026-11-06). `--wcag` checks pages against WCAG 2.1 AA by default, with `--wcag-version` (`2.0`, `2.1`, or `2.2`) and `--wcag-level` (`AA` or `AAA`), and `--best-practice` to add the 27. A rule is chosen by its criterion tags (`wcag143` is 1.4.3) through `wcag.within()`; deprecated rules never run, and experimental ones only with `--experimental`.

**axe-core 4.14.0's experimental rules run only when named by rule.** They're `td-has-header`, `table-fake-caption`, and `p-as-heading` (1.3.1), from 2.1 `css-orientation-lock` (1.3.4), and with `--best-practice` `focus-order-semantics` and `hidden-content`. Run by rule (`runOnly`), axe-core runs them, where its default leaves them out of a run by tags; `--experimental` adds them among those chosen (2026-11-07). On a page with no stylesheet at all, `css-orientation-lock` is undecided.

**axe-core reports a link inside a hidden caption as `aria-hidden-focus`.** Pandoc's HTML writer hides a figure's caption that repeats its picture's alt text (`<figcaption aria-hidden="true">`), a note's mark with it. Measured 2026-11-09 on a page of a caption with two notes, with the filter as it was and with one that hides the caption's words and leaves its marks: axe-core 4.14.0 found `aria-hidden-focus` at the `figcaption` with the old filter and nothing with the new; Chromium 141's accessibility tree, read through Playwright 1.56, holds both marks' links in the figure with the new one and neither with the old, and names the figure nothing either way (Playwright's own computation names it "12" with the new).

### Running it in Playwright's Chromium

**The prototype of `--wcag` on pages ran axe-core 4.14.0 in Playwright 1.63's Chromium 153 headless shell** (November 1, 2026), on Ubuntu 24.04 with two processors and 8 GB, as root and as a normal user, with Pandoc 3.13 building real pages and presentations with `convert.py`. What's marked measured 2026-11-01 here was measured with it.

**`axe.run()` in the page checks only the page, not its frames.** Measured 2026-11-01: it missed a picture without alt text in a frame served from the folder. So the worker runs `runPartial()` in each frame and `finishRun()` in a blank page, as axe's [Playwright integration](https://github.com/dequelabs/axe-core-npm/tree/develop/packages/playwright) does. It does this only in frames served from the folder: run in every frame, it reported a missing title and language for the error page a refused YouTube frame shows (measured). A refused frame is noted as not checked.

**axe's partial results have to cross Python as JSON text.** Passed as objects through Playwright for Python, a check's `undefined` result came back as `null`, and `finishRun` gave an element axe couldn't decide a failure's message (as built, 2026-11-06).

**axe-core can't decide the contrast of an element that holds only non-text characters, which a formula's operators are.** On a page built by hand the way TextbookImprover builds pages, axe found no violation and left 18 elements undecided for contrast, with the same reason for every one, "Element content contains only non-text characters": 15 MathML operators (minus signs and sums) and 3 footnote back-links (↩︎) (measured 2026-11-01). The real pages built from the test suite's Word fixtures had none; their one formula has only parentheses, an equals sign, and a bar. The summary groups such `axe-review` findings by axe's reason (its `messageKey`, `nonBmp` here), so a book's thousands of operators make one line.

**`color-contrast` is axe-core's expensive rule, and it runs a second time only on a page that changes in dark mode.** Pandoc 3.12's stylesheet follows the reader's color scheme, and `page.css` gives dark mode its own colors with `light-dark()`, which the unit suite checks against fixed backgrounds. When a page's computed `color-scheme` includes `dark`, `color-contrast` runs again with Playwright emulating `prefers-color-scheme: dark`, and those findings say "in dark mode".

**What axe-core costs a page** (measured 2026-11-01, with two processors and 8 GB):

- A page like a TextbookImprover section (29 KB, three tables, six formulas, three notes) takes 0.50 s, one at a time, counting the context, the load, and axe. Two at a time it's 0.35 s a page, and four or eight at a time is no faster. At that rate a 400-page book's pages would take about two and a half minutes on that machine. The worker checks as many pages at once as there are processors, up to four.
- axe on that page takes 0.40 s, nearly all of it `color-contrast` (0.08 s without it).
- A 242 KB page with 60 tables and 120 formulas takes 3.2 s for axe, and 2.7 s more for contrast in dark mode.
- Six real pages, built with `convert.py` and Pandoc 3.13 from the test suite's Word fixtures (15 or 16 KB each), take 0.04 to 0.06 s each for axe, and 0.01 to 0.02 s more for contrast in dark mode, which their stylesheet supports. axe found only the pictures without alt text that the fixtures have, in light and dark mode alike.
- A presentation takes about 1.5 s for 11 slides and 4.8 s for 41, after the first load that lists its scripts.

The 29 KB and 242 KB pages were built by hand the way TextbookImprover builds pages, with `page.css` inlined, and the table wrapper, landmarks, and menu as `figures-and-tables.lua` and `convert.py` write them, from Pandoc 3.1.3, so their numbers aren't from a run of the pipeline; the fixture pages' and the decks' are.
