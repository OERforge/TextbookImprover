# CNX notes

A reference on OpenStax's CNXML sources and how `bin/unpack-cnx.py` reads them: the format and OpenStax's repositories, cookbook's recipes and what the unpacker does in their place, the exercise bank, provenance and licenses, what was measured against OpenStax's own conversions, and what converting the books found. The user's account is [`docs/cnx-input.md`](../docs/cnx-input.md); this file is the reference behind it, and repeats a fact from it where the reason or the measurement is the point. What each book measured when converted is in [CORPUS-NOTES.md](CORPUS-NOTES.md); what Pandoc and LaTeX do with the unpacker's pages is in [PANDOC-NOTES.md](PANDOC-NOTES.md) and [LATEX-NOTES.md](LATEX-NOTES.md). Each entry says how its fact is known and when, as [README.md](README.md) describes.

## The sources: CNXML, collections, and OpenStax's repositories

### OpenStax's repositories

**OpenStax's books are in English, Spanish, and Polish, and cookbook has words in those three languages alone.** Of the 78 books in the 46 repositories cloned (counted 2026-10-27), 61 are in English, 11 in Spanish, and 6 in Polish, and the approved-books list (openstax/content-manager-approved-books at 93a3a9a) names twelve more repositories, eleven by English titles and one holding the same two Spanish statistics books. Cookbook (v2.33.0, f5b488f) has 53 locale files in English, 14 in Spanish (one its tests'), and 6 in Polish. What a Spanish or a Polish book is baked with is under Words by language, below.

**Four styles' books have no public repository, so their ports are checked on cookbook's samples alone**: _Introduction to Computer Science_, _Clinical Nursing Skills_, the Polish _Marketing - Podstawy_, and the Polish nursing book (`pl-nursing`, whose sample's text is English). `computer-science` is _Introduction to Computer Science_ (`osbooks-introduction-computer-science`), and `nursing-internal` is _Clinical Nursing Skills_ and, by OpenStax's list of its books ([the ABL](https://corgi.ce.openstax.org/api/abl/)), _Fundamentals of Nursing_, _Maternal-Newborn Nursing_, _Medical-Surgical Nursing_, and _Psychiatric-Mental Health Nursing_ (`osbooks-nursing-internal-bundle`); _Clinical Nursing Skills_ and _Introduction to Computer Science_ are under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, their prefaces say. Three ways to such a book were weighed (2026-10-29): OpenStax's Word files, which their help page says each section has for a verified instructor's account ([licensing and customization](https://help.openstax.org/s/article/Openstax-textbook-licensing-and-customization)), and which the Word corpus has for _Clinical Nursing Skills_, with the Word files' known faults (Measured against other conversions, OpenStax's Word files); the repositories themselves, or a copy, from OpenStax; or OpenStax's baked pages, which their web reader loads from `openstax.org/apps/archive/` (openstax/content-ruby's `Archive`, MIT), a reader for which would be new, which robots.txt asks every crawler to leave alone, and which no document of theirs describes. A book whose source isn't public is converted from OpenStax's Word files, as any Word book is (`docs/cnx-input.md`, since 2026-10-30). The two Polish books are set aside, English and Spanish being what's produced (2026-10-20).

Of 21 repositories cloned and unpacked with `main` at f8b885a, all 24 books unpacked, in 0.2 to 14 seconds; five were converted to pages, an EPUB, and a PDF, all five making pages and EPUBs and three PDFs. Found, each on the roadmap for the archive's work (`ROADMAP.md`, The Connexions archive, later) but the last: _Communication Systems_' PDF stops at a Mathematica CDF file its `<media>` names as the web's image (`application/vnd.wolfram.cdf`, beside a PNG for print), which `choose_media` takes since it isn't for print; a link with no target and no text is written as the word "link" (`text_for`'s `href or "link"`), "Chapter IIlink delineates…" in _Patricia Martin's PhD thesis_; the Vietnamese book's PDF stops without babel's Vietnamese, and its figures are numbered "Figure 1" beside its own "Hình 5.1"; and _Natural Sciences Grade 7_'s build warns that the PDF's tables don't line up with the book's 71, so it retags no caption (not traced).

## Cookbook's recipes, and what the unpacker does in their place

### Notes

**An OpenStax book's notes are boxes, each headed by its kind and its own title in one heading.** Baking makes a note's kind, its label, a heading and its title another under it, and a heading with only one heading below it makes no sense. A label that isn't read as a heading and is still accessible would have served, but there isn't one in every format. HTML has `<aside>`, which a screen reader announces as a landmark named by its label (`aria-labelledby`); PDF 2.0 has an Aside structure type, but its label could only be a paragraph, Title being for a document's or a chapter's title and Lbl for a label inside another element, a list item's number or a heading's (ISO 32000-2, 14.8.4); and Word has neither. A paragraph is read, but a screen reader's list of headings passes it over. So the label and the title are merged into a single heading. A space baking leaves at the label's end, its divider's after a number (_Calculus_' theorems, "Theorem 2.1 ") or its string's (the statistics books' "Stats Lab "), is taken off, where it had stood before the colon in 287 headings, which a review found; the punctuation test hadn't seen it, `text_of` evening out spaces. And a title's own id, which OpenStax's assembly writes and the port's baking doesn't, stays, an anchor in the heading, as an id the label held does. Unpacked again (2026-10-20), the 67 books' 16,034 pages hold 32,414 notes: 30,527 boxes and the 1,887 references (_Principles of Management_, _Organizational Behavior_, and _Introduction to Business 2e_); 7,484 notes had been headed by two headings and are headed by one, 6,835 merged, 583 by the label alone (579 of _Physics_' "Teacher Support"), and 66 by the title; 21,814 have one heading, as before, and 3,116 none (the references, 981 equation callouts, and the like); and no page differs outside its notes. What a note's heading is, case by case, is in `docs/cnx-input.md`.

**Notes are `div`s, titled by a heading.** `<aside>` gains nothing: Pandoc reads it as a `Div` with class `aside`, and writes that back.

**A note whose kind the locale doesn't name has no label**, where cookbook writes "unknown title for note with classes [...]": the Polish nursing sample's special considerations, safety alerts, and trending today, which its strings lack.

### Glossaries and indexes

**The glossary and index sort is CLDR's root collation as twitter_cldr gives it.** Cookbook sorts by the book's locale with TwitterCldr's collator (`lib/kitchen/patches/i18n.rb`), and the port's order was checked against twitter_cldr itself on all 12,934 terms of the eight repositories cloned at the time: identical order. Text is sorted as it stands, spaces included, so a term written with a leading line break sorts first, as OpenStax's does. Index entries merge across capitals exactly as cookbook's `item_for` does (Ruby's `capitalize` lowercases the rest, so "Gross domestic product (GDP)" met first doesn't merge with "gross domestic product (GDP)"); the one change is that whitespace is evened out before matching.

**An index's groups are cookbook's**: a term is listed under its first letter as Ruby's `I18n.transliterate` spells it in ASCII, its accent off, capitalized as Ruby capitalizes (Æ under "Ae"), and a character with no such spelling, a digit, a sign, or a letter of another script, under Symbols: _Biology 2e_'s "α-helix", which an accent-stripping port had put under a group of its own, "Α". Cookbook groups a term by its first letter without the accent in every language but Polish (`I18n.character_to_group`).

**A term a chapter's Key Terms has twice is one term with both meanings**, a dt and two dds, the second term's ids kept at the head of its meaning. Cookbook lists both definitions, each its own dl (two sections define "pancreas" in _Concepts of Biology_'s chapter 16, and "psychosocial development" in a chapter of _Psychology 2e_); the Key Terms are one dl here, which shouldn't name a term twice. Found by converting those books with validators on: listed twice in one list, each was the Nu checker's "Duplicate “dt” name".

**A Polish book's terms are in three indexes**, of subjects, of names (`cxlxt:index="name"`), and of words in other languages (`foreign`, each with its language), listed by the form the source gives for the index (`cmlnle:reference`, "wrażenia zmysłowe" for "wrażeń zmysłowych"; a name's `cxlxt:name`, "Darwin, Karol"); grouped by that form's first character as it stands, Ł a group of its own; and sorted, with the glossaries, by CLDR's Polish collation (ą after a, ł after l, ź then ż after z), which is twitter_cldr's, cookbook's collator, for Polish.

**A Polish meaning that is one paragraph keeps its markup.** Cookbook's `has_para` keeps the first paragraph's text alone, its italics and formulas lost, any later paragraph too; here the paragraph's content is the meaning's, and its id is kept. A list item that is one paragraph (BakeListsWithPara) is its content as cookbook makes it, the paragraph's id kept as well.

### Words by language

**A book in Spanish is baked with cookbook's Spanish words** (e942d17, 2026-10-27): `COMMON_ES`, cookbook's `lib/locales/es.yml`, and `BY_STYLE_ES`, the `locales/es.yml` of each of the twelve recipes that has one (anatomy, calculus, chemistry, computer-science, finance, history, microbiology, political-science, precalculus, precalculus-coreq, statistics, u-physics), each word as cookbook has it, for the keys the port's English has, merged over the English for a book in Spanish by `for_style`, as the Polish are. Glossaries and indexes are sorted by CLDR's Spanish collation, ñ a letter after n (`&N<ñ`, twitter_cldr's tailoring for `es`, which cookbook sorts with), a decomposed ñ as the composed one (`OWN_LETTERS` in `directions.py`, which holds the Polish letters too); an index lists a term that opens with ñ under N, as cookbook does. And the footer `conversion.yaml` gives an OpenStax book is in the book's language, cookbook's `folio.access_for_free` ("Acceso gratis en openstax.org", "Podręcznik dostępny za darmo na openstax.org"), with the period the license page's English has: a Polish book's had been English. Compared with cookbook's files, the port's Spanish and Polish are the same word for word, but "Problema", which cookbook has no Spanish for (`docs/cnx-input.md`); cookbook's words for what the port doesn't do (link descriptions, the table of contents' title, an iframe's link text, `folio.preface`) aren't ported, in any language.

**A Polish book's links name their targets in the case the sentence wants.** The source marks a link's case (`cmlnle:case="genitive"`, which the unpacker keeps as `data-case`), and the Polish recipes label what a link can name in all seven cases ("Ilustracji 1.2", "na Ilustracji 1.2"); a link that names none shows the nominative, as a label in the text does. The strings are cookbook's `pl.yml`, the English ones where a Polish one is missing (cookbook writes "translation missing").

### Links

**An address written as text is a link to itself**, as cookbook's `post_bake` makes one after every recipe (302 in _Principles of Economics 3e_'s references; OpenStax's Word file has 303 hyperlinks there), so `convert.py` reports them as bare links as it does the Word file's. A Connexions archive book's are left as text, and so is an address a typo has made invalid (a port that isn't a number, a backslash, no host, a host ending in a number that isn't an IPv4 address, a period for `org/`), which `post_bake` links: the link goes nowhere, and an EPUB holding it fails epubcheck. A host that ends in a number (`https://doi.10.1111/bcp.13499`, `https://doi.org.10.7326/…`, a period for a slash) is what the URL Standard rejects: linked, 1 Nu checker error in _Pharmacology_ and 7 in _Population Health_, every one in the 41 repositories cloned. What such an address holds that a link's can't is escaped in the link, the text left as written: a `%` that starts no escape (_Lifespan Development_'s `#:~:text=In%`, an address a line break cut), `{`, `}`, `|`, `^`, `` ` ``, `[`, and `]` (_Principles of Accounting_'s `#{%22issue_id%22:390208,…}`), and a `#` after the one that opens the fragment (_Principles of Marketing_'s `…?form_type=&year=1994##document-2239…`, the second written `%23`; linked as it stood, it was a Nu checker error and two of epubcheck's). And what cookbook's pattern takes in after an address that isn't part of it is text after the link: a closing quotation mark and the punctuation before it (“https://docs.google.com.”, a link to a host that isn't one, five in _Workplace Software and Skills_, others in the _Writing Guide_, _Introduction to Intellectual Property_, and _Population Health for Nurses_), and a mark that shows nothing (a left-to-right mark after a lecture's address in the statistics bundle); and an address whose host has an empty or impossible name (“http://.”, the _Writing Guide_'s advice to leave it out, which linked was two vnu errors and two of epubcheck's) isn't linked. Of the 14,881 links the port makes of addresses written as text in the repositories cloned, 15 change, in 13 texts: 12 end where their address does, and 3 that went nowhere aren't made.

### What isn't written

**What cookbook writes for screen readers alone isn't written** (BakeScreenreaderSpans: "underline" and "end underline" around each underlined letter of _SohCahToa_), since a page without OpenStax's stylesheet would show it; the underline itself is kept. **Folios aren't written** either (BakeFolio's running heads for print, "1.13.4 • Creating a Table from an Equation" on each page, which OpenStax's stylesheet hides on the web).

### Numbering and titles

**An unnumbered table keeps its title and caption** in _Astronomy 2e_ and _College Physics_, whose recipes number every table (BakeNumberedTable.v1), counting the unnumbered ones as OpenStax's books do, and take an unnumbered one's caption away, putting nothing back (_Astronomy 2e_'s "Table A").

**Learning objectives numbered by their place are a numbered list** (2026-10-17). A conversion of _Principles of Marketing_ showed a bullet and a number for each of its "Learning Outcomes" (Rob, 2026-10-17): BakeLearningObjectives.v2 writes each objective's number as text (`span.os-abstract-token`) in a `ul.os-abstract`, whose bullets OpenStax's stylesheet hides. Where the numbers count the objectives, 1, 2, 3 (`count_only_li`: marketing, pl-marketing, and marketing's appendix), the list is an `<ol>`, the numbers its own and the token gone; where they're the section's (`in_chapter`, "1.1.1": calculus, algebra-1, data-science, neuroscience, nursing-external, pl-nursing), it stays bulleted with each number as text, which an `<ol>` can't show, and is left so for now (2026-10-18). `util/cnx-compare-word.py` reads an `<ol>`'s numbers as words, as the Word files have them.

**_Algebra 1_'s units are numbered as its table of contents numbers them** ("Unit 1 Linear Equations"), not as BakeUnitTitle numbers their headings, counting the three unnumbered units too ("Unit 3"). OpenStax's reader shows the contents' numbers, never the headings. Its lessons are its chapters ("Lesson 1.2"), each page numbered from its overview (1.2.0), as the recipe numbers them; the units and lessons that open, support, and close the book, and each unit's overview and projects, go unnumbered, and the lessons after them are numbered as if they weren't there (Kitchen counts an element among those its search found). A unit's own page is named by the unit's number (`1-unit-1-wrap-up`), where it was its place.

**_Organic Chemistry_'s problems open "Problem 1-1"** (`problem_with_prefix`), the words and number one link to the solution, run into the problem's first paragraph as any number is.

**_Principles of Accounting_'s chapter 8 nests a Thought Provokers section inside its Problem Set B** (module m67878), so its exercises are numbered twice and prefixed "TP" and "PB", as cookbook bakes them too. Left as cookbook has it.

**_Marketing - Podstawy_'s references are footnotes of class reference**, numbered through each chapter, each a superscript number linking to its entry and the entry a paragraph opening with the number, linking back ("1."), on the chapter's references page (BakeFootnotes.v1 with its selector and BakeChapterReferences.v4); its other footnotes are Pandoc's notes, numbered as the notes settings say, where cookbook numbers them in Roman. A chapter with no references gets no references page; cookbook makes it empty.

### Single books' pages

**The _Writing Guide_'s retitled pages and sections keep their headings as their sections have them.** Cookbook's BakeCustomSections titles a Trailblazer page by its kind ("Trailblazer"), its own title an h3 under it, and puts an h3 naming a section's kind ("Living by Their Own Words") at the head of the section, its own title made an h4. Here both are paragraphs, the kind's just before its section, whose title stays its heading: as headings, one put a level between a page's title and its sections, the other a section's title under a heading that isn't its section's. A retitled page is named by its own title (`1-2-social-media-trailblazer-selena-gomez`), as OpenStax's contents, made before the retitling, name it. The icons OpenStax's stylesheet draws beside the handbook's annotations, with their names for screen readers alone, aren't written.

**A reaction's mechanism is captioned as any figure is** (_Organic Chemistry_'s class `mechanism-figure`): cookbook sets its label, number, and title above the mechanism and its caption beside it, for print; here it's the figure's caption, after the figure as every one is.

**Chemistry Matters' page closes up its dash** ("Chemistry Matters—Organic Foods: Risk versus Benefit", UseSectionTitle), as _Organic Chemistry_'s text writes its dashes; OpenStax's page shows a space either side, the line breaks of cookbook's templates.

## How the unpacker reads CNXML

### Pages, titles, and headings

**Pages are named as OpenStax names them** (its Word files and web addresses), checked against the Word files of the corpus's six books: 1,379 of 1,386 names match, and the other 7 were the answer key pages of _Principles of Data Science_, empty without its exercises from the bank (with them, the book's 112 pages were compared on 2026-11-04, and no page of its answer key is below 0.98; Measured against other conversions, OpenStax's Word files). The rules: section `3-1-…` from its numbered title; introduction `{n}-{title}`; end-of-chapter page `{n}-{title}`; answer key page `chapter-{n}`; appendix `{letter}-{title}`; a unit's own page `{unit}-{title}`. Slugs: lowercase, apostrophes dropped, accents stripped, `®` as `r`, everything else a hyphen.

**A page's title is its first `h1`**, the introduction's included (OpenStax puts it after the opening figure and outline), and **a table's caption is a `<caption>`** at its top.

**Footnotes are Pandoc's** (`role="doc-noteref"`, a `role="doc-endnotes"` section), numbered through each chapter, as OpenStax numbers them, by `notes.numbering: group` in `conversion.yaml`, each page keeping its own at its end; a unit is `type: part` in `project.yaml`, so each of its chapters numbers on its own, and a page at the top level, a preface or an appendix, is a group of its own (`main`'s `notes.groups`, 1192f59). Measured, HTML and EPUB, on _Entrepreneurship_ (its 15 chapters with notes, chapter 1's four pages with them numbered 1 to 4, 5 to 20, and 21 to 24) and _Concepts of Biology_ (11 chapters with notes in six units, Chapter 7 and Chapter 8 of Unit 2 each from 1): every chapter numbered from 1 through its pages, and the EPUB's numbers the pages'.

**The word a caption's own label opens with is the book's**, written into `conversion.yaml`'s `captions.figure_prefixes` and `captions.table_prefixes` where it isn't TextbookImprover's `Figure` or `Table`: _Principles of Management_'s and _Introduction to Business_'s "Exhibit", a Polish book's "Ilustracja" and "Tabela", from the style's strings. A PDF then sets each such caption with its own label and none of LaTeX's (since 2026-10-19). Converted with and without it, _Principles of Management_'s and _Makroekonomia - Podstawy_'s pages are the same; the Polish book's 16 tables whose caption is "Tabela" and a number alone, with no summary to describe them, are reported in `table-captions-missing.csv`, as an English book's are, where they weren't before.

**Section titles are `h3` (depth 1) and `h4`, other titles `div[data-type=title]`**, as OpenStax's assembly writes them, so the recipes' selectors find what theirs do; `emit.py` sets the levels pages are written with.

### Table summaries and labels

**A table's summary is its drafted description** in `table-captions.csv`, marked drafted by OpenStax: under its label where the caption is the label alone, and under TextbookImprover's positional key (`chapter-12#table-5`) where there's no caption at all, as for the unnumbered tables in _Principles of Economics 3e_'s answers. The unpacker counts a page's tables as `figures-and-tables.lua`'s `number_tables` does (document order, a table before those in its cells, a table of pictures skipped). _Principles of Economics 3e_ has 23 such tables, and after converting their pages each description was on the table whose own summary it is. A summary that says nothing isn't drafted: OpenStax's stand-ins, "No Summary" (575 in the repositories), "Assessment data" (124, with and without its period), "Assessment information" (34), "summary" (21), and "no alt text" (13), and what alt text's checks call a placeholder, punctuation alone (671): 1,438 of the 6,214 summaries in the 41 repositories (June 2026), which is what a review counted. And 166 more, found listing the summaries by how often each is written: _Principles of Finance_'s "Some cells are left blank for visual design purposes." (75), which says why a cell is empty and not what the table holds, the chemistry books' "key equations table" (51), "Table" and the like (26, "Table...." among them), "table x.x" (5), "No-Summary" (5), and a Polish template's "The table is called...Opis tabeli" (4): 1,604 in all. Summaries that say little but something are drafted ("empty x and y table", 9 in the algebra books). _Life, Liberty, and the Pursuit of Happiness_'s 434 summaries all say nothing (416 "No Summary", 17 ".", one "--"), so its boxes are no longer captioned "No Summary"; the run counts them. Nor is a layout table's summary drafted, which asks for none.

**A summary that reads its table out is drafted as any other is, and named** (2026-10-16; the rule widened 2026-10-17). Such a summary ("Two rows and five columns. The first row is labeled, “x”, and the second is labeled, “g(x)”. The values of x are 2, 4, 6, and 8…"), drafted as a caption, is read before the table it repeats. Deleting them by default was the worse choice, so long as they can be found reliably: each is in `table-captions.csv` with its words as they are, where they're easily changed, marked as OpenStax's, and the report names it (`table-summary-reads-out`, its key and its first twelve words), for a person to put what the table shows in its place; `cnx-table-summaries.csv` marks it in a column, Reads out. `reads_out` (`cnx.project`) names a summary that reads its table's rows or columns as ordered pairs, a row's or a cell's words ("Top left cell reads: Essential Knowledge", "Row 2 has “Big Bang” and 10 to the 68"), its shape or a column's name and then a run of its values (four numbers or three quoted words), a header's numbers ("are the numbers: 0.10, 0.20, and 0.30"), or a function's values ("f(1)=6, f(2)=8, f(3)=3, and f(4)=1"), in English or Polish; and one that repeats its table's own cells in words of its own ("Washington 67, J. Adams 90, Jefferson 83…", `repeats`, given each table's body cells by the emitter): the words of six cells, or five that are half the table's, from two columns or more, compared whatever their case, dashes, quotation marks, or spacing, a letter alone not counted and a number inside another not taken for a cell's. Of the 4,610 summaries that say something in 41 of OpenStax's repositories (June 2026), it names 1,941, 1,283 of them on a table with no title, whose summary is drafted: 1,609 by their words and 332 more by the cells they repeat (the CNXML's cells, measured). Read: 70 of those its first words name, and all 29 the numbered rows, the headers' numbers, and the functions' values add, and every one reads its table out; and 50 of the 332, of which 49 do, the other reading out a stem-and-leaf plot's stems. Below those thresholds it named too much: at three or four cells, 6 of the 25 it would add were descriptions, figure grids' labels among them ("A 2x2 grid displaying four graphs labeled Graph A, Graph B, Graph C, and Graph D"); at five of sixteen, one whose digits were the table's ("Values of algebraic expressions (x, x^2, x^2+5, x^2-4)…"); and from one column, a list of the rows' names. In the 16 books with Key Equations, unpacked, it names 951 of the 1,020 summaries drafted (894 by the first words: 30 of those were read, and 25 of the 57 the rest add, and every one reads its table out). Of the 69 it doesn't name, all read, 41 read their tables out in ways it doesn't know ("“Pythagorean Identities” with three cells. First: sin(theta)^2 + cos(theta)^2 = 1…", "For (x,f(x)) we have the values (2, 4) and (5, 12)", house values of "37700" where the cell has "$37,700"), and 28 say what the table is ("Chart of year vs membership fees", "empty x and y table"). ### Code

**Code is a block where OpenStax's assembly makes one**: anywhere but in a paragraph or a list (nebuchadnezzar's `cnxml-to-html5.xsl`), or in a title, a caption, a term, and the like, unless it says `display="block"`; code of class `inline-code` is inline, as their stylesheet shows it (`pre.inline-code { display: inline }`, for a table's cells). A block is `<pre><code>`, losing the blank line it opens with, the indentation it closes with, and the indentation all its lines share (the markup's); a newline element followed by a line break is one line break (Python writes both for a program's output, and in a `<pre>` a browser shows two). `line-numbering` adds Pandoc's `numberLines`, since the text cites line numbers. A block holding bold or italics (_Introduction to Python Programming_'s example input, 14 of them; _Principles of Finance_'s `=SLOPE(`_y_`-data range, …)`) is a `<p>` of inline code with `<br>`s, its indentation no-break spaces, since a code block would drop the bold. A colored span in code (_Principles of Data Science_'s `green-code`, _Principles of Finance_'s `red-code`) is left to be dropped: color alone. Before the rule, code standing apart was written inline, so Pandoc ran each program's lines together: 350 blocks in _Introduction to Python Programming_, 243 in _Principles of Data Science_, 24 more in _Principles of Finance_, 4 in _Foundations of Information Systems_.

**A paragraph that holds nothing but code of more than one line is a block of code, class `inline-code` or not** (2026-10-29), which amends the rule that such code is inline anywhere. _Introduction to Python Programming_'s Table 8.2 has a program and its output in its cells, each a `code` of class `inline-code` alone in a `para`, its lines `newline` elements or line breaks, which the unpacker wrote as a paragraph of code, and which Pandoc's HTML reader read as one line, the run of spaces the output is about one (PANDOC-NOTES, The HTML reader); a list's item in 1.1 holds the lines a reader is to add to a program so. `cnx.cnxml`'s `lone_code` finds such a paragraph, no text of its own and one code element of more than one line, not said to be inline, and `do_code` writes it as a `pre`, the paragraph's id and language its, the class `inline-code` dropped; the code's own id, where it has one, is the `pre`'s, and the paragraph's then a division's around it. **A program's language is a class of the `<pre>` too.** OpenStax names a program's language as a class of its code element (`python`, `r`), and Pandoc's HTML reader doesn't merge a `<pre>`'s classes with its `<code>`'s, the `<pre>`'s winning where both have one (PANDOC-NOTES, The HTML reader), so the unpacker puts the language on the `<pre>` as well, and no block of the books loses it.

## The exercise bank

### Fetching and the cache

**A link `#exercise/NAME` is fetched as `?q=nickname:NAME`, and `#ost/api/ex/TAG` as `?q=tag:TAG`**, from `https://exercises.openstax.org/api/exercises`, cached raw under `~/.cache/textbookimprover/cnx-exercises/{public|with-token}/{kind}/{code}.json`, eight at a time. `--offline` reads the cache only; `OPENSTAX_EXERCISES_TOKEN` is sent as a bearer token.

**Fetching is part of unpacking** (2026-11-04): `unpack-cnx.py` fetches what a book embeds as it puts the book together, the pages it writes hold the exercises, and `convert.py` never asks the bank for anything. An exercise in the cache isn't fetched again, so the cache isn't brought up to date on its own; `--update-exercises`, added since, asks the bank again. A machine without the network unpacks from a copied cache with `--offline`. `docs/cnx-input.md` says so.

**Formulas in exercises are TeX** (`<span data-math="…"></span>`, _Principles of Data Science_'s solutions among them), made MathML with `pandoc -f latex` at unpack time, asked for MathML in the form the installed Pandoc takes (`--mathml` before 3.11, `--math-method=mathml` from it, `pandocversion.mathml`), without a line break just before an environment's end, which MathJax ends no row at, all in one call and one by one where that call's count doesn't match; one that fails is read again with MathJax's and plain TeX's forms written as LaTeX's (`lib/cnx/texforms.py`), and one that fails both ways stays TeX and is reported (as of 2026-11-04).

**OpenStax's text faces are larger and sturdier than Latin Modern at a given size, and three of its four heading faces draw I and l alike** (measured 2026-10-20), the question being whether copying OpenStax's faces would serve accessibility, which had already been chosen over fidelity to its design in the type's size. From a survey of the research, checked where it mattered: print size is the best-supported factor in legibility, measured by x-height (Legge and Bigelow 2011; UKAAF's 2024 guidance gives 2 mm for 12-point clear print); at equal x-height, typefaces differ little, serif or sans, and fonts designed for low vision or dyslexia haven't beaten good ordinary ones; ordinary weights do alike, and the one low-vision study that varied the contrast between thick strokes and thin found low contrast best; and readers differ, the font they prefer not the one they read fastest (Wallace et al. 2022). Latin Modern's thick strokes are 3.8 times its thin ones, Plex Serif's about 1.9, the sans faces' 1.2, and its hairlines a third of a pixel at 12 points and 100% zoom. ## Traps for whoever ports the next recipe

**A line break between elements in one of cookbook's templates is text.** Cookbook writes its markup in templates whose line breaks fall between elements: `<span class="os-title-label">Think It Through</span>`, a line break, `<span class="os-number">1.1</span>`. That line break shows as a space ("Think It Through 1.1"); a port that appends the two spans shows "Think It Through1.1". The samples can't catch it: cookbook's `scripts/normalize` reads the expected output with `noblanks`, dropping every space between elements, before its tests compare, so `cnx-compare-cookbook.py` counts each element's edge as a break and can't tell. Found so in BakeNumberedNotes.v1's heading and BakeExercisePrefixes's prefix ("TP 1.", as openstax.org shows it); fixed. When porting, look at each template for elements on lines of their own.

**A word the locale doesn't have is written out as such**: a note of a kind its strings don't name is titled "unknown title for note with classes [...]", and a string missing from a Polish recipe's `pl.yml` would read "translation missing" (cookbook's I18n has no fallback to English). Look at a recipe's note classes against its locale.

**Kitchen's `search(a, b)` returns what the first selector finds and then what the second does**, not the two in the order they stand: _World History_ numbers a page's own exercises and then its bank questions, wherever each stands. A port that takes `a, b` as one XPath union gets document order and numbers them differently; the samples catch this one.
