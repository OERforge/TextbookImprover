# Corpus notes

The books and decks TextbookImprover is tested on: for each, where it comes from and under what license, how it's built, what it exercises, and what converting it measured, with the date and the versions. Measurements go stale as the code changes, so each is dated, and a later one replaces an earlier one unless the change itself is worth keeping. The books named are openly licensed and public. A few samples were lent for testing and aren't public: those are described, not named, and nothing of their content is given beyond counts. What OpenStax's CNXML sources are and how they're read is in [CNX-NOTES.md](CNX-NOTES.md). [README.md](README.md) describes how an entry is written.

## The Word corpus and the table census

**The Word corpus is nine books in 1,782 files, holding 3,030 tables, 1,045 of them data tables** (measured with Pandoc 3.11): OpenStax's Word downloads of _Principles of Economics 3e_, _Introductory Business Statistics 2e_, _Principles of Marketing_, _Principles of Data Science_, _Introduction to Sociology 3e_, _Clinical Nursing Skills_, and _Foundations of Information Systems_, a non-OpenStax business communication text (one file a chapter, `BC-01` to `BC-16`), and an introductory programming text in one file. `util/slim-corpus.py` makes the copy the census is run on, the slim corpus, each `.docx` reduced to its `word/document.xml`, which is all the census reads. The census's guess gave 477 `both`, 466 `first-row`, 65 `first-column`, 20 `unknown`, and 17 `none`; those figures move whenever a rule changes, so re-derive them rather than cite them. The comparison across Pandoc versions was run against 3.1.3, 3.9, and 3.11 side by side.

**The slim corpus has no pictures, so a table report made from it runs high** (2026-10-15). Its 1,980 picture tables come out as empty data tables, all but one a single cell, not figures, and inflate any table report (21 on one appendix page of _Principles of Economics 3e_): compare builds, not counts. Most layout-looking tables in the OpenStax books are numbered tables the text cites. Of the 29 corpus tables a test's `layout` rows declared, 26 kept a head, since Pandoc's Word reader puts row 1 in the head when Word repeats it as a header (20) or its table look formats it (the programming text's 6).

**No table of the corpus opens with a band, a row, and a band** (2026-10-13, all 3,030 tables). The census takes a merged first row over a header row for the table's title only where that row heads every group (`heads_every_group`), and otherwise for the first band. The rule before it (`main` at ac683cc) made a band in the head over a header row the title whatever was below, which got _Clinical Nursing Skills_' Table 12.2 wrong: two tables in one, its second part headed by the first's header row over its own. The other shape the rule decides, an unmarked header row under the title with the first band right under it, which the earlier rule split at the title, is in no corpus table. Against the earlier rule the census changes one corpus table, 12.2, back to `split-at` 1,6, and of the 37 files converted end to end only that page changes; the five split tables with a band in their head (_Foundations of Information Systems_ 3.5 to 3.7, _Clinical Nursing Skills_ 12.2 and 4.4) all come out right.

**The table fixes of 2026-10-13 changed 8 guesses on seven of the corpus's books**, the pre-pass run on them with `main` at 01df4ec and at ac683cc (2,943 tables in 1,766 files, 947 prefilled rows). The 8 are the 6 notes across a table's foot (`split-at` no longer names them; _Principles of Economics 3e_'s Table 11.1 takes its first row as its title, `caption-rows` 1) and 2 merged titles over a band in the head Pandoc reads (_Clinical Nursing Skills_ 4.2 and 12.6, `caption-rows` 1 where `split-at` had named row 1). 13 tables have a band in that head (row 1, formatted by Word's table look and marked to repeat as well, in all 13, and the header row under it marked too in 8): those 3, 1 in _Introduction to Sociology 3e_ and 6 in _Introductory Business Statistics 2e_ (each a title, `caption-rows` 1), and 3 in _Foundations of Information Systems_ 3.1 (database tables in normal forms: "Beginning Data", then its own header row, then the next band and its header row), guessed as the first band, which is right, each band heading its own header row. The shape left as a known gap, a merged title over a header row over bands with the first part the header row alone, is in none of the 17 tables whose first row is a band with more bands below: each is a group's band over rows of its own (_Principles of Economics 3e_'s 7.14 and 17.3, _Clinical Nursing Skills_' lab values, and the like).

**Three quarters of the alt texts in the corpus's first three books are over 120 characters** (for 0.8, 2026-10-03; not measured again since _Principles of Data Science_ was added). 1,002 images carry alt text: median 210 characters, mean 283, longest 3,853, and 766 of them, 76%, over 120. 257 have MathSpeak alt text, meaning an equation was stored as a picture; on those the length check fires and the audit's score drops, yet shortening the alt text would make the content worse and leave the defect, which is fixed by writing the equation again in the source. The opposite failure turned up in the HTML and EPUB sources: alt text that is only the image's file name, which passes a presence check and describes nothing. `image-alt-is-file-name` reports it.

**The deck rules for alt text name little in the corpus** (2026-10-13, `lib/alttext.py` on the slim corpus's 2,462 descriptions). One is Office's own ("AI-generated content may be incorrect."), and the page-title rule took seven labels for titles (Creative Commons' "Attribution - By", and "Knowledge Check - Memo" in the business communication text's `BC-08` and `BC-09`). So a one-word name after a dash counts only after three words or more, which names none of the seven and loses none of the rule's 66 matches in the 827 distinct decks measured then.

**None of the Word corpus's headings holds a formula** (2026-11-06), so the corpus doesn't exercise a page's title made from a formula in its heading.

### Minus signs and other characters

**The micro sign (µ, U+00B5) stands for mu in 75 of _Introductory Business Statistics 2e_'s formulas** (from its CNXML, 2026-10-14). `math.repair_equations` makes it Greek μ.

**OpenStax's books write a minus sign in text as an en dash, 2,166 times in ten books** (2026-11-06). Measured with the pass alone (`math.minus_in_text`; no equation repaired, no math made of text) on the intermediates of _Principles of Economics 3e_'s and _Introductory Business Statistics 2e_'s runs and on the pages the CNX unpacker made of eight more, _Astronomy 2e_, _Biology 2e_, _Chemistry 2e_, _College Algebra 2e_, _Intermediate Algebra 2e_, _Physics_, _Principles of Financial Accounting_, and _Statistics_: 2,166 minus signs, 793 of them in _Chemistry 2e_ and 574 in _Statistics_ (`docs/configuration.md` has each book's count). Each of the 277 distinct differences written with spaces was a subtraction, read one by one; one more had been a range, _Chemistry 2e_'s `n = 2 – 5` (its _MX_ molecules), which the rule for a range of whole numbers after a relation, with nothing after it, now leaves. The signs read in samples were signs, after a list's comma (`{0.2, –4.1, –1.6}`), a numbered answer (`75. –66.4 kJ`), a part's circled letter (`ⓒ–8`), and in alt text (_Physics_' energy levels).

**What the pass leaves, of the en dashes beside a number in text**: ranges, nearly all of them; a sign with a space after it, past a word (`the image is – 2.00 cm`, `are – 0.811`), since a spaced dash there is a sentence's dash as often (`False – 50 percent of the data`); a difference with no relation after it (`400 – 320, or 80`, _Principles of Economics 3e_'s 7.3; `n – 1 degrees of freedom`; `($8,000 – $1,500 – $120)`) or with an operand a run doesn't take (`(r – 1)(c – 1)`, `(°F – 32)`, `4.0331 amu – 4.0026 amu`); OpenStax's typo `6 -– 2`; and an en dash in an equation's own text (`\text{~–~}`), which the equation repair left a dash until 2026-11-07 (next). In a run, with `math.from_text` on, an expression of italic variables, `P(x > 2) = 1 – P(x ≤ 2)` or `q = 1 – p`, is made an equation first, its en dash its minus sign (measured on a page made for it).

**An en dash in an equation's own text (`\text{}`) is a minus sign where the text is math** (2026-11-07): a number's sign, a charge's, a difference, as _Principles of Economics 3e_ types its equations (`\text{16~–~2P~–~2}`); a text that's the dash alone, with space, is the operator. It stays a dash between two letters (a bond, `H–Cl`), between two digits (a range), beside another dash, and in a text set under or over something (_Intermediate Algebra 2e_'s `\underset{\text{–}}{x}`). On the 150 distinct formulas of eight OpenStax books whose text holds an en dash, the equation repair makes 258 minus signs and 31 operators and leaves 9, five chemistry bonds and intermediate algebra's marks; in samples of each book's, every one changed was math.

### Lines too wide in a PDF

**The widest lines found in an OpenStax book's PDF are a worked example's steps and a statistical table** (2026-10-21, `main` at bec2e48, each built with its pages widened to show what runs off them). _College Algebra 2e_'s first chapter, 136 pages in Noto Sans and 128 in Latin Modern, has 28 lines too wide in Noto Sans (4 display formulas, 24 lines of text) and 14 in Latin Modern (1 and 13), the widest by 224 and 136 points as TeX measures a line, its box against the text's width; every one is a formula's, and none a line of prose or a table. The "lines of text" are worked examples' steps, which OpenStax writes as one formula in a paragraph, a table of each step and its reason, the reason after a space in the formula (in the chapter's pages, 60 spaces of 4em or more, the widest five of `<mspace width="13.5em"/>`, in section 1.2's (f)), and which TeX can't break, so a reason too long for the line runs off it. The widest, section 1.2's (d), "Commutative and associative laws of multiplication", after a space of 3em, has its last letter 218 points past the text's right edge in Noto Sans and 130 in Latin Modern, past the paper's edge in both.

**A formula is larger beside Noto Sans than beside Latin Modern**, which is why the same chapter has more lines too wide in it. Pandoc's template matches every face's small letters to the text's (PANDOC-NOTES), so beside Noto Sans, whose small letters are taller than Latin Modern's, Latin Modern Math and the em of a formula's spaces are 24% larger (in the PDF, Latin Modern Math at 14.87 points beside the text's 11.96). Of the 88 points more that section 1.2's (d) runs over by in Noto Sans, 65.5 are its formula's and its space's, which start its reason further right, and 22.6 its reason's, longer in Noto Sans (2026-10-21).

**_Introductory Business Statistics 2e_'s statistical tables are the widest tables** (2026-10-21, the same builds). Its appendix A is 22 pages in Noto Sans and 21 in Latin Modern, with 40 and 36 lines of its tables too wide, the widest by 172 points in both: Table A5 (F critical values), thirteen columns at the same places in either face, its last four past the text's edge and the last two wholly past the paper's. Since these builds, a worked example's steps written as one formula are unpacked as a table whose reasons wrap (`docs/cnx-input.md`), and a table or formula too wide for its line is fitted to it (`docs/formats.md`); the figures in these three entries are from before both.

### axe-core's WCAG findings
