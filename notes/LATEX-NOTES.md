# LaTeX notes

What LaTeX, its packages, and LuaTeX do, as read from their source or established by test, for the questions this project keeps asking. Separate from PANDOC-NOTES.md because it's about LaTeX, not Pandoc. Read this before answering a "what does LaTeX do when…" question, and read the source before running an experiment. The facts were read and measured in Debian's TeX Live 2026 packages as of Ubuntu 26.10's snapshot of 2026-07-11 (LaTeX 2026-06-01, l3kernel 2026-06-18, tagpdf 1.0c, luamml 0.9.2), the tree whose files are listed below, and, from 2026-11-10, in TinyTeX alone: its 2026.09 bundle as released (LaTeX 2026-06-01) and the same bundle brought up to date (LaTeX 2026-11-01). TinyTeX's bundle, brought up to date with `tlmgr update --self --all`, has later releases of some of these files (tagpdf 1.0g or 1.0h, latex-lab 2026-06-01a, l3kernel 2026-09-09), so each entry says which release a fact was read or measured at, and whether it was read from the source or measured. What TextbookImprover does about a fact is named at its end: the LaTeX a PDF from the pages gets is in `lib/latexbuild.py` (`PDF_MACROS`, `FIT_MACROS`, `PICTURE_PATHS`, `UA1_LINKS`, `CODE_SPACES`, `UM_ALIASES`, `FLOATS_HERE`), `bin/build-pdf.py`, `bin/pdf-target.lua`, and `bin/footnote-code.lua`, and what a LaTeX book's tagged copy gets is in `lib/texremediate.py`. Where a fact is Pandoc's output meeting LaTeX, the Pandoc half is in PANDOC-NOTES.md.

## Where things are

## Releases and resources

**A tagged build needs LaTeX 2026-06-01.** The tagging project describes tagging as usable in production from the release of 2025-11-01, for documents that keep to packages that support it ([its site](https://latex3.github.io/tagging-project/)), but measured with every suite on `main` at 01df4ec, Ubuntu 26.04's TeX Live 2025 (LaTeX 2025-11-01, tagpdf 0.99x, luamml 0.7.0) fails 14 checks where Debian's TeX Live 2026 (LaTeX 2026-06-01, tagpdf 1.0c, luamml 0.9.2) fails none. Two builds stop: a PDF/UA-1 book, whose `math/setup={mathml-AF,mathml-MS}` 2025-11-01 doesn't take ("The key '__tag/setup/math/__setup/mathml-MS' is unknown"), and a tagged document with enumitem's `leftmargin=*` ("Package block Error: Some keys specified on the itemize environment are unknown"). The rest build wrong: a table cell's formulas get no Formula and no MathML, and an `eqnarray*`'s rows are one Formula without a MathML file; figures' tags are gathered at the end of the document, `float/here` not existing yet; the enumerate package's labels repeat ("(a) First part (a) Second part") and a theorem's heading loses its period; and a source copy's `\begin{picture}[alt=…]` stops pdfLaTeX. Ubuntu 24.04's TeX Live 2023 (LaTeX 2023-11-01) failed 28, and no LaTeX release falls between 2025-11-01 and 2026-06-01. `latexbuild.MINIMUM_LATEX` is 2026-06-01, and `latexbuild.latex_problem` reads the release LuaLaTeX runs from `\fmtversion` and refuses an older one (measured 2026-10-19).

**An older LaTeX can't open a tagged document at all.** Older releases load `pdfmanagement-testphase.sty` for `\DocumentMetadata`, where current ones load `pdfmanagement-init` (`documentmetadata-support.ltx`, read: its copy of 2026-06-15 requires `pdfmanagement-init`), and Ubuntu 24.04's packages (LaTeX 2023-11-01) stop on that file not found (`latexbuild.py`'s comment on `MINIMUM_LATEX`); Ubuntu's 2023 `lualatex` doesn't know `\DocumentMetadata`'s `tagging` key. A TeX Live 2026 installed before its June update has LaTeX 2025-11-01 until `tlmgr update --self --all`, and TeX Live 2025's LaTeX stopped at 2025-11-01 (`docs/installation.md`).

**LaTeX 2026-11-01 changed what the code's fixes read, and they read both releases now.** The release (on CTAN 2026-10-31, LaTeX News 44) reimplements headings as templates, with new structure names (`chapter`, `chapter-heading`) and separators in place of `number-title-sep`, the AMS classes' headings and theorems among them; renames paragraphs' tags (`semantic-para`, `text-block`, `text-fragment`); revisits the block environments' paragraph handling and splits their `legacy-code` key; begins an emulation of titlesec; and gives template keys names of their own (read in LaTeX News 44 as CTAN's latex-dev folder has it). Measured on `main` at 4e162e6, 2026-11-10, with TinyTeX brought up to date (the kernel and latex-lab at TeX Live's rev. 80868, tagpdf 1.0i, luamml 0.9.3, babel 26.15, fancyvrb 4.8, hyperref 7.01t, l3kernel 2026-09-09) and with the 2026.09 bundle as released (LaTeX 2026-06-01, latex-lab 2026-06-01a, babel 26.11, fancyvrb 4.7, hyperref 7.01r, the same tagpdf and luamml): the LaTeX suite failed 11 checks on `main` under the first and none under the second. In what its cases leave under the first: `\keys_set:nn { template / block / std } { left-margin = … }`, as `texremediate.ENUMITEM_KEYS` writes it, stops LaTeX ("The key 'template/block/std/left-margin' is unknown and is being ignored"); an item that opens with fancyvrb's code has no label at all, where `cmd/FV@ListVSpace/before` had set it beside the code; a book-class chapter's heading reads "Chapter  1AlphaoneAlphatwo" in its structure, and a section's "1.2  Ensec Entitle", two after its number, where 2026-06-01's read "Chapter 1 Alphaone Alphatwo" and "1.2 Ensec Entitle"; a tagged copy built as a DVI stops on "pdfTeX error (\pdffakespace): not allowed in DVI mode" at `\part`; and amsart's contents set a section's number under its title. Under it, too, the PDF suite failed 18 of 383 checks that pass under 2026-06-01, veraPDF's PDF/UA-2 profile on its notes book among them, and the decks suite an item that opens with highlighted code. Each was traced (2026-11-10) and the fixes made to read what the release in use has: enumitem's settings and the list templates' keys (Lists and their labels, below); a list's label before fancyvrb's code (the same); a heading's spaces and a DVI's `\pdffakespace` (Paragraphs and sections, and tagpdf's spaces); a heading's link target after its notes, which outlasted the notes under both releases (Footnotes); and, for the rest of the PDF suite's 18, the suites' own reading of structure the release names otherwise (LaTeX 2026-11-01's names, below). Measured on the fixes, every suite passes under both trees, but for the decks suite's 4 that fail under both on `main` too.

**The run is tested with LaTeX 2026-06-01 and 2026-11-01, and warns of a newer one.** Each release has changed what LaTeX's tagging names and how it sets what it tags, and the fixes here reach into that code, its internals among it (`\l__tag_para_bool`, `\g__block_labels_box`, the templates' keys), so `latexbuild.TESTED_LATEX` is the newest release tested, 2026-11-01, and `latex_problem` warns of a newer one, naming both, and builds with it; the installation page names the two releases and the trees they were measured in.

**pdfLaTeX keeps a tagged document's structure in TeX's main memory, which a book outgrows.** GIAM with `\DocumentMetadata` doesn't build with `pdflatex`: TeX's main memory (5,000,000) runs out, so a tagged book is built with LuaLaTeX, a change to the author's build (measured, 2026-10-05). Untagged, the same tree builds with pdfLaTeX. `texremediate` takes out what forces pdfTeX: the `pdftex` option of `\documentclass`, `\usepackage`, and `\RequirePackage`, and settings such as `\pdfcompresslevel=9`.

**LuaTeX grows its main memory as it goes.** A test that only grew the page never got "TeX capacity exceeded": LuaTeX grew until it was killed, and Pandoc's log ended without the error; a million names fill LuaTeX's strings in 5 s, and `\def\a{\a\relax}\a` fills the input stack (measured, 2026-10-05). A large tagged book can outgrow strings, hash, or memory, since tagging keeps every structure element (Prescott, TUGboat 47:2). `latexbuild.failure_advice` names the table that ran out and the variable that raises it (`max_strings`, `hash_extra`, and `extra_mem_top` and `extra_mem_bot` for pdfTeX's main memory, in `texmf.cnf` or the environment; [tex.stackexchange.com/a/741777](https://tex.stackexchange.com/a/741777/)), and calls an overflowing stack a macro that calls itself.

**Tagging costs time per pass, not passes.** GIAM takes 5 s a pass with pdfLaTeX, 7 s with LuaLaTeX, and 52 s tagged (2026-10-05); `latexmk -lualatex` ran LuaLaTeX four times on the tagged copy (235 s), as the author's Makefile runs pdfLaTeX four times. MathML adds more: with MathML as structure elements and attached files the copy built from clean in 481 s, with the attached files alone 257 s, and without MathML 226 s, so the structure elements cost most of it (2026-10-06). A Java programming textbook in LaTeX: 11 s a pass untagged, 75 tagged.

**A LuaLaTeX run on a large book or chapter can take 3 GB.** _Instrumental Analysis_'s PDF needs about 2.6 GB, and _College Algebra with Corequisite Support 2e_'s chapter 7 took 2.9 GB, measured where a shell's commands had about 5.8 GB (2026-10-23 and 24); _Introductory Business Statistics 2e_, built whole, took 3.2 GB, and two whole books built at once ran out of that memory (2026-10-29 and 30). A run the kernel kills ("Memory cgroup out of memory") leaves its `.aux` cut off, and the next run stops at `\begin{document}` ("File ended while scanning use of \@newl@bel", or "File ended while scanning definition of \LT@cclxiii"), so whole books are built one at a time, and a build that fails so is run again alone before it's taken for a fault of the code.

**A class or package loaded inside a group stops LaTeX.** `\@fileswithoptions` raises "Loading a class or package in a group" when `\currentgrouplevel` is above zero (read in `latex.ltx`, 2026-06-01). _OpenIntro Statistics_' `style.tex` opens a brace on line 346 that nothing closes, so hyperref and biblatex, loaded at `\begin{document}`, stopped its build, a check releases before 2025 didn't make (measured 2026-10-07); `latexsource.brace_problems` names such a brace by file and line.

## LaTeX's tagging code

Read in TeX Live 2026's latex-lab (the block module 1.0c of 2026-06-10; enumitem's emulation 0.80f of 2026-04-21; the table module 0.85t, the graphic module 0.80i, the toc module 0.85k, and the footnote module 0.8p of 2026-04-28; the float module 0.81n of 2026-04-24; the math module 0.7e of 2026-05-27; firstaid 0.85x of 2026-06-08), tagpdf 1.0c (2026-05-17), and pdfmanagement 0.97c (2026-05-26), and measured with LaTeX 2026-06-01, unless an entry says otherwise.

**LaTeX 2026-11-01 names a paragraph's structure and a heading's otherwise.** In `tagpdf-ns-latex-lab.def`, `text` is `text-block` (P), `text-unit` `semantic-para` (Part), `section-number` (Span) `heading-number` (Lbl), and a span of text `text-fragment` (Span), where it was a Span; a level's name, `chapter` say, is its section (Sect, or Part for `part`), and its heading `chapter-heading` (H1, or Title for `part-heading`), where `chapter` was the heading inside a `Sect`; a heading's paragraph is `heading-fragment` (Span), where it was a Span; a minipage and a `\parbox` are `text-minipage` and `text-parbox` (Div), `heading-minipage` and `heading-parbox` (Span) in a heading, where they were Divs; and the title block is `title-block` (Div), `title-text` (Title), `title-author`, and `title-date` (P), where it was `title` (Title) (read in both trees). The suites read either release's PDF as 2026-06-01 named it (`pdftext.as_before`, the PDF suite's `kind` and its walkers), or accept both names (`pdftext.TEXT` and its kin; 2026-11-10).

### Lists and their labels

**An item's label waits in the tagging code's own box until the item's first paragraph begins, so a list's item that opens with highlighted code loses its number.** LaTeX's tagging code for lists (`latex-lab-testphase-block.sty`) keeps an item's label in `\g__block_labels_box`, not in `\@labels`, and sets it when the item's first paragraph begins (the `para/begin` hook, `\__block_item_everypar_std:`); `\item` adds its label to that box with `\hbox_gset:Nn`, unpacking what's there first. fancyvrb's list begins no paragraph: it sets `\@inlabel` false (`\FV@ListVSpace`) and puts `\@labels`, empty under tagging, beside its first line (`\FV@ListProcessLine@i`), and latex-lab's first aid for fancyvrb (`latex-lab-testphase-firstaid.sty`) keeps that. Pandoc writes an item that opens with highlighted code as `\item` and, on the next line, `\begin{Shaded}` around fancyvrb's `Highlighting`, `Shaded` empty unless the highlighting style has a background, when it's framed's `snugshade`, which isn't involved. In a tagged PDF the item's label waited, and the next `\item`, finding `\@inlabel` false, added its own to the same box, so both were set at the next paragraph, "2." over "3."; the structure had each label in its own item's Lbl. Untagged, `\@labels` holds the label and fancyvrb sets it in place, and an item that opens with code in no language, LaTeX's verbatim, which sets each line as a paragraph, has its number either way. Read in the block module 1.0c and fancyvrb 4.6a, and measured 2026-10-28 with Pandoc 3.13 and TeX Live 2026. `latexbuild.PDF_MACROS` moves a waiting label to `\@labels` before `\FV@ListVSpace` clears `\@inlabel` (`cmd/FV@ListVSpace/before`, added once fancyvrb is loaded), only where a label waits and only where the tagging code's box exists, so a fix of LaTeX's own isn't undone; a LaTeX book's tagged copy doesn't get it, and no book here needs it. Not among the problems the tagging project's issue on fancyvrb lists ([latex3/tagging-project#741](https://github.com/latex3/tagging-project/issues/741)). A LaTeX file of 16 lines shows it with LuaLaTeX and pdfLaTeX, with LaTeX 2026-06-01 and with the latex-dev pre-release ("2026-11-01 pre-release-0 (develop 2026-10-24 branch)"), and the same hook in that file's preamble fixes it with both engines; LaTeX's own `verbatim` in its place keeps the number (measured 2026-10-28). Not reported upstream as of 2026-11-11; a web search found no report of it, and the tracker's own search wasn't tried. LaTeX 2026-11-01's block module (1.0v, 2026-10-27) makes `\@labels` its own box, `\cs_set_eq:NN \@labels \g__block_labels_box`, so fancyvrb sets a waiting label where it waits, and moving it to `\@labels` and clearing the module's box cleared it: the item had no label at all (measured with the LaTeX suite's case). `CODE_ITEM_LABEL` does nothing where the two are the same box (`\cs_if_eq_p:cN { @labels }`, since `@` isn't a letter in the code; 2026-11-10).

**An untagged build sets an item's label from `\everypar`.** A box's rule that cleared `\everypar` while a list's item, a quotation's, or a definition's label waited for the item's first paragraph stopped an untagged build ("perhaps a missing \item"), found by a review (2026-10-20). `\OERBoxRule` leaves `\everypar` alone while `@inlabel` is true.

**`\item`'s optional argument is read as keys when it holds an `=`.** The block module redefines `\item` at `begindocument/before` as `\RenewDocumentCommand{\item}{ ={label}o }`, so an argument with an `=` in it is a list of keys, and keys it doesn't know stop LaTeX: "Package block Error: Some keys specified on the \item command are unknown" (read in the block module 1.0c). A description list's term is `\item[term]`, so a term such as "x = 1" stopped the PDF until Pandoc 3.13 braced it (jgm/pandoc#11954); one whose `=` is in math, code, a link, or emphasis builds, its argument a command's (measured with 3.12.1, 3.13, and LaTeX 2026-06-01, 2026-10-26). `pdf-target.lua`'s `term_braces` braces such a term for an older Pandoc.

**Lists are laid out by an emulation of enumitem, which lacks most of enumitem's keys.** The block module replaces `enumitem.sty` with nothing (`\declare@file@substitution{enumitem.sty}{null}`) and loads `latex-lab-enumitem.sty` (0.80f, 2026-04-21), whose keys map to the block templates' own: `leftmargin` is `left-margin` and `labelwidth` is `label-width`, dimensions, so `leftmargin=*` and `labelwidth=*` stop LaTeX ("Missing number"), and `style`, `labelindent`, `widest`, `left`, `ref`, `label*`, `before`, `after`, and `series` aren't keys of it or of the block module's list template, so LaTeX stops on them as unknown ("Some keys specified on the itemize environment are unknown"). Read in TeX Live 2026's files; measured on FINC 308, whose 96 lists use `leftmargin=*` (2026-10-06). For a LaTeX book's tagged copy, `texremediate.ENUMITEM_KEYS` defines each missing key: a width to compute or a label's indent is left to the emulation's defaults, `resume` continues the numbering, `nolistsep` is `nosep`, and code around a list, a nested label, and `\ref`'s form are left out; it does nothing where enumitem itself is loaded. LaTeX 2026-11-01's emulation (0.80i, `\ltlabenumdate` 2026-10-24) has more: `leftmargin`, `itemindent`, `labelsep`, `labelwidth`, and `labelindent` each take `*` and `!`, computing the width from the others, `*` as `!` with a warning ("The key value `*` is not supported. Value `!` instead used for key `leftmargin`"), and `ref`, `font`, `format`, and `align` are keys of it; `widest`, `left`, `label*`, `before`, `after`, `series`, and `style` are still not (read in `latex-lab-enumitem.sty` of both trees). And the templates' own keys aren't l3keys of `template/<type>/<name>` there, so `\keys_set:nn { template / block / std } { left-margin = … }`, as `ENUMITEM_KEYS` had written it, stopped LaTeX ("The key 'template/block/std/left-margin' is unknown"), where `\SetTemplateKeys { block } { std } { … }` sets them in both releases (measured). `resume` is the list template's own key in both, a boolean, which continues the numbering as given; with a series' name, enumitem's `resume=name`, 2026-06-01 stops ("Key 'template/list/std/resume' accepts boolean values only"), and 2026-11-01 builds, the item's label a U+FFFD, tagpdf warning "Parent-Child 'pdf2:L' --> 'pdf2:Part'. Relation is not allowed!" and "'pdf2:L' --> 'pdf2:P'", and a description list after it numbered "1." (measured with a sample of lists, both trees, 2026-11-10). Why, as read 2026-11-10 (not reported upstream as of 2026-11-11): 2026-11-01's template code takes any value for a boolean key, `\__template_assign_boolean_aux:n` putting `\exp_not:c { bool_set_ \l__template_value_tl :N }` and the variable among the template's assignments (`latex.ltx`, line 8593 on), so `resume=steps` is `\bool_set_steps:N`, which `\exp_not:c` makes `\relax`, and the variable after it, a `\chardef` of 0 when false, is typeset: "Missing character: There is no ^^@ (U+0000)", the U+FFFD being `pdftotext`'s for it. Any template boolean the same, `\begin{itemize}[legacy-support=xyz]` setting "1." on a line of its own and the missing character before its item; 2026-06-01's code checked the value, its error l3keys' own for a boolean key. `ENUMITEM_KEYS` sets the templates' keys with `\SetTemplateKeys`, leaves `leftmargin=*`, `labelindent`, and `ref` to the emulation where `\ltlabenumdate` is later than 2026-10-23, and defines `resume` in both, a name or not, as continuing the numbering; `convert.py`'s message says which release takes which.

**The book class numbers a one-sided book's title page and the page after it both i.** `book.cls`'s `titlepage` sets the page counter back to 1 at its end when the class isn't two-sided (lines 514–516, read 2026-09-26), so in any `oneside` book the title page and the page after it are both i. ### Footnotes

**A note's text is a link target that outlasts the note.** latex-lab's footnote code, which `\DocumentMetadata` loads with tagging on or off, makes a note's text a target with `\MakeLinkTarget*{footnote*.N}` (`fntext/mark/link`, `latex-lab-footnotes.ltx`), and `\MakeLinkTarget` sets `\@currentHref` globally (`\tl_gset:Ne`, `latex.ltx`), so after a note `\@currentHref` is the note's: Pandoc's `\section[Sets]{\texorpdfstring{Sets\footnote{…}\footnote{…}}{Sets}}\label{…}` wrote `\contentsline {section}{\numberline {1.1}Sets}{3}{footnote*.4}` and `\newlabel{…}{{1.1}{3}{Sets}{footnote*.4}{}}`, its bookmark to `footnote*.4` too, the last note's text at the foot of the page, with LaTeX 2026-06-01 and 2026-11-01 alike, untagged with `\DocumentMetadata` too, where without it hyperref's own footnote code, which sets `\@currentHref` inside the note's group, kept `section.1.1` (measured 2026-11-10). ### Floats, captions, and graphics

**LaTeX's tagging gathers floats' tags at the end of the document unless told otherwise.** The float module keeps each float's structure in a container of its kind, placed at the end of the document or where `float/flush` puts it: `\g__tag_float_sect_bool` is true by default (`float/defer`), and `float/here`, its inverse, tags each float where its `\begin{figure}` or `\begin{table}` is, ending and restarting the paragraph around it, without moving it on the page (read in the float module 0.81n). veraPDF can't tell: 91 of GIAM's 195 figures, those in floats, were in two containers after everything else (2026-10-06), so were 246 of the statistics textbook's 270, and a floating table between two paragraphs of a book's first chapter was tagged after its second chapter (2026-10-22). The key came in latex-lab-float 0.81m (2025-12-04), after LaTeX 2025-11-01. `latexbuild.FLOATS_HERE` sets it where the key exists, for a book's PDF and, through `texremediate.FLOATS_HERE`, a LaTeX book's tagged copy. placeins alone moves a figure on the page and not its tags, which stayed in the container at the end of the document (measured by 2026-10-02), so before it set `float/here` the target's `pdf.figures: section` was placeins' `[section]` with `\tagpdfsetup{float/flush=section}` (and `=chapter`) before each sectioning command; a bare `float/flush` failed on the contents page's `\chapter*`, where `\@currentseclevel` is undefined, so the level was given. Under `float/here` a figure's tags are in place whatever `pdf.figures` says (`docs/formats.md`).

**A tagged figure's caption is LaTeX's own, which the caption package doesn't reach.** The float module redefines `\@makecaption` at `begindocument`: the label is the `caption/label` socket's, whose kernel plug is `#1:~`, between two tagging sockets, `caption/label/begin`, which switches paragraph tagging off and opens a Lbl, and `caption/label/end`, which closes it and switches paragraph tagging on (read in the float module 0.81n). So LaTeX labels every caption, and a book whose captions number themselves had two numbers ("Figure 1: Figure 1.1 The real number line"), each read by a screen reader. `\OERNoCaptionLabel` (`PDF_MACROS`) gives the label socket its `noop` plug and the two tagging sockets plugs that only switch paragraph tagging off and on, so the Caption holds its words and no empty Lbl; made `noop`, those two had left paragraph tagging one paragraph short at a caption too long to center. A longtable's caption, and an untagged figure's in a book with tables, is the caption package's, told to give none (2026-10-19).

**With the caption package, a list of figures' or tables' entry refers to nothing in the tagged PDF.** LaTeX's toc module gives each entry's TOCI a `/Ref` to the structure element of the target its `\contentsline` names, which tagpdf has where `\MakeLinkTarget` made the target (`\g__tag_struct_dest_num_prop`, filled at `cmd/MakeLinkTarget/after`); the float module makes a float's own, `figure.struct.N`, and its `\caption` sets `\@currentHref` to it, so in a document of LaTeX's own a list of figures' TOCI refers to the float. The caption package, which Pandoc's template loads in a book with a table, sets its own target for the caption, `figure.caption.N` (with `hypcap=false`, `figure.N`), which has no structure, so tagpdf warns "Destination 'figure.caption.4' has no related structure" and the TOCI's `/Ref` is empty, for every figure and float table; a longtable's entry refers to its Table (measured with a document of LaTeX's own, `\usepackage{caption}` the only difference, and a Markdown book through the PDF target, both trees, 2026-11-10). veraPDF's PDF/UA-2 profile passes them, and the entries' links work. Not fixed or reported: the tagging status list rates the caption package currently incompatible, and recording the caption's target would reach into tagpdf's property list.

**A caption short enough for one line is set in a box as wide as the line, where a line break spreads it across the line.** The float module's `\@makecaption` measures the caption in a box, its tagging suspended, and where it's no wider than the line sets it again in `\hb@xt@\hsize{\hfil` … `\hfil}`, the label and the text inside, not the box it measured (read in the float module 0.81n; latex-dev's pre-release of 2026-11-01 and TinyTeX's copy are the same). A `\\` there is `\unskip\nobreak\hfil\break` in an hbox, and its `\hfil` takes a third of the line's room: in a document of LaTeX's own, tagged, `\caption{Short\\caption}` set "Figure 1.1: Short" and "caption" 78.79 points apart, and "Geological Time Chart" and "(credit: Richard S. Murphy, Jr.)" 17.30 apart (measured with `pdftotext -bbox`, 2026-11-03). Untagged, the kernel's `\@makecaption` sets the box it measured, at its own width, where the `\hfil` has none, so the words run together, "Shortcaption". The caption package, given the same caption, measures it in a box and, where it fits, sets it centered, with `\\` a `\@centercr`, so it's two centered lines; LaTeX's tagging doesn't reach a figure's caption with it, and a longtable's caption is still its own, two centered lines tagged or not (measured the same day). OpenStax's books have 43 captions with a line break (`<newline/>`, which the CNX unpacker writes as `<br>`), in 16 repositories, most a title and a credit on a line of its own, *Biology 2e*'s "Geological Time Chart" among them (counted in the CNXML, 2026-11-03). `LINE_BREAK_SPACE` measures a caption holding `\\` or `\newline` before LaTeX does (`cmd/@makecaption/before`), its line breaks as nothing, its tagging suspended, the footnote counter set back; where it fits a line, the caption's line breaks are its own until the caption ends: in LaTeX's measuring box a line break is a glue as wide as the line, so LaTeX takes the branch it sets a long caption in, a paragraph, and there a line break is a forced break without the `\hfil`, the paragraph's lines centered by `\leftskip` and `\rightskip` of `0pt plus 1fil` and no `\parfillskip`, as the caption package's are. Once the paragraph begins (`para/begin`), a line break in a box inside the caption (an `\mbox`) is LaTeX's own, which the box's filter makes a space. A caption longer than a line was a paragraph already, which its line break breaks. A line break at a caption's end, nothing after it, `\caption{Nu Xi\\}` or Pandoc's caption ending in `<br>`, is nothing: set as such a paragraph, it had left an empty line under the caption, the text after the figure 9.9 points lower (found in review, 2026-11-03), and in LaTeX's own box its `\hfil` had pushed "Figure 1: Nu Xi" off center, 45.6 points left of where "Figure 2: Nu Xi" without the break was centered (measured 2026-11-03). The measuring finds it: each break leaves a penalty of its own in the measuring box, and the box ends with one (`\lastpenalty`); LaTeX meets each break once in each pass over the caption, measuring and setting, so that break is the last of each pass's count. Where it was the caption's only break, the caption is set as one with none, centered: in LaTeX's box, a paragraph's end (`para/textblock/end`, `latex.ltx`) takes the last glue off with `\unskip`, the line's width a last break leaves there with it, where LaTeX's own `\\` ends with a `\null` that keeps its `\hfil` from it (read, and found by a mutation that counted the breaks without the modulus, which changed nothing in a one-line caption); so the count is for a long caption, set as a paragraph, whose last break left an empty line, as LaTeX's own does. And a note's mark in a caption is numbered twice under tagging, on `main` too: the float module sets the caption's text twice, measuring it and setting it, and `\footnotemark` steps the counter each time, so `\caption{Rho Sigma\protect\footnotemark}` and its `\footnotetext` number the document's first note 2, each such caption's two more, where untagged they're 1, 2, 3 (measured 2026-11-03, no list of figures). A Markdown book's caption isn't touched, `pdf-target.lua` opening one that holds a note with `\OERCaptionNotes`, which sets the counter back each time after the first (`latexbuild.PDF_MACROS`); a LaTeX book's `\footnotemark` in a caption is, left as LaTeX's own. The caption's own measuring sets the counter back after it, so the numbers are LaTeX's, the LaTeX suite's check comparing a caption with a line break to two without. Not found in latex3/tagging-project's tracker by a web search: a candidate, the measuring box being the one to center. Not reported upstream as of 2026-11-11.

**An image without alt text is tagged as a figure described by its file name.** With `\l__tag_graphic_alt_tl` empty, the graphic module's `alt` plug warns "Alternative text for graphic is missing. Using '…' instead." and makes the file name, made plain, the figure's `/Alt` (`alt-text-missing`, read in the graphic module 0.80i); a drawing gets `picture environment`. veraPDF accepts either, so the output check reports a figure so described (`pdf-figure-alt-is-file-name`), and `pdf-target.lua` marks a decorative image with the graphic module's `artifact` key, in a group (`\OERArtifactGraphics`); `alt={}` after `artifact` sets the image back to a figure. Under Pandoc's `-t pdf`, as a book's PDF is built, the file name is the image's path in Pandoc's temporary folder, whose name is random, so it differs from run to run (measured by 2026-11-06, not run again since; PANDOC-NOTES, Reproducible builds).

**The graphic module keeps the alt key until it's set again.** It holds the key in `\l__tag_graphic_alt_tl` and clears nothing after an image, and `alt={}` is the state a graphic starts in, so `\setkeys{Gin}{alt={…}}` before a call of a book's own image macro and `\setkeys{Gin}{alt={}}` after it describe the image inside without a group, which would have ended a `\centering` in the macro before its paragraph did and restored `\@currentlabel` before a `\label` after the call (2026-10-06). But `\setkeys` in an argument LaTeX writes to a file or keeps to set elsewhere (a caption, `\captionof`'s and `\subcaption`'s too, a heading, either one's short form, `\index`, `\thanks`, `\title`, `\author`, `\markboth`, `\markright`, and `\addcontentsline`'s entry) stops the build, with pdfLaTeX and LuaLaTeX alike, and `\includegraphics`'s keys can't hold a paragraph break ("Paragraph ended before \Gin@ii was complete"; measured, `docs/latex.md`). `texremediate` writes the keys at each call and names each image it can't.

**graphicx defines `alt`, `actualtext`, and `artifact` itself, and older releases don't all know them.** graphicx 1.2e (TeX Live 2026's file is dated 2026-05-17 and latex-dev's 2026-06-26, the same version; its first date wasn't found; read 2026-10-28) defines `alt` and `actualtext` as keys that do nothing and `artifact` as a boolean key, and the graphic module defines all three again at `package/graphicx/after` (read), so the keys build without `\DocumentMetadata` on LaTeX 2026-06-01. TeX Live 2023's graphicx, 1.2d of 2021/09/16, knows `alt` and stops at `artifact` ("artifact undefined"); `\picture` takes options first in LaTeX 2026-06-01, so `\begin{picture}[alt={…}]` stopped TeX Live 2025's pdfLaTeX; and a `tikzpicture`'s `alt` and `artifact` were defined only by the tagging code's TikZ module until pgf 3.1.12 (2026-08-01), which defines them as keys that do nothing, waiting for tagging to give them a use ("I do not know the key '/tikz/alt'" otherwise). `texremediate.KEYS_SKIPPED`, before a LaTeX book's `\begin{document}`, gives each missing key one that does nothing and leaves the kernel's `\picture` alone unless it's the one that takes no options; checked with TeX Live 2023, 2025, and 2026, with pdfLaTeX and tagged with LuaLaTeX.

**An included PDF's fonts are the page's, and PDF/UA's rules on fonts reach them.** Measured on a tagged document of 121 figures, each in a Figure with alt text (2026-10-29; `docs/latex.md`, Measured): veraPDF's PDF/UA-2 profile failed it on the fonts OpenIntro Statistics' R figures don't embed (8.4.5.5.1, 115 checks) and on mapping their glyphs to Unicode (8.4.5.8, 30); with their fonts embedded by Ghostscript, as `latexbuild.EMBED_GS` writes a figure, on one glyph of one figure, the PDF Ghostscript made of a matplotlib EPS, a Type 3 font's glyph that maps to no Unicode; and with each figure an SVG made a PDF by `rsvg-convert`, which draws the text as paths, not at all. So a LaTeX book's PDF figures have their fonts embedded before they're included, in the PDF it builds from its own LaTeX and in one from its pages, and a glyph with no Unicode is the output check's veraPDF's to report.

**graphicx's natural size under LuaLaTeX**, which its `scale` multiplies: a PDF's page's crop box as the file writes it, its media box where it has none, turned as the page is (a page 400 by 300 points turned 90 degrees is 300 wide), and a crop box larger than the media box set at its own size, where `pdfinfo` clips it to the media box, as the PDF reference has a reader show the page; an EPS's `%%BoundingBox`, as `epstopdf` makes its PDF's page without `--hires`, as LaTeX's epstopdf package runs it; a PNG's pixels at its pHYs resolution, which is per meter, rounded to whole dots per inch (5,925 a meter, 150.495 an inch, is 150), or at 72 to the inch where it has none, or one after its image data; a JPEG's at its Exif resolution, per inch or per centimeter, rounded to whole dots per inch (118 a centimeter is 300), which LuaTeX takes over a JFIF one, larger or smaller, an Exif resolution of no unit passed over for the JFIF one and an Exif with no unit given read as inches; else at its JFIF resolution, a resolution per centimeter made per inch with its fraction dropped (118 is 299), or 72. A resolution of 0 either way, or of more than 65,535 to the inch, is 72 both ways. Measured against `\includegraphics` in TeX Live 2026 with 15 images made for it, three of GIAM's scaled images, two photographs and a PNG, and 25 more a review made, ten of which found the last four rules (2026-10-30).

**A book's PDF draws an image at that natural size, no wider than its line, and carries a JPEG's bytes as they are.** Pandoc's LaTeX writer puts an image its page gives no width in `\pandocbounded`, which shrinks it to the line and the page and leaves a smaller one at its natural size, and `pdf-target.lua` puts one given a width or a height in it too; so *Principles of Marketing*'s 800-pixel photographs of 200 pixels an inch are drawn 4 inches wide, and its chapter openers, 1,750 pixels wide, across the default page's line of 6.5 inches, at 269 pixels an inch (r42's `scripts/placed.py`, which reads each image's drawn width from the pages' content streams, 2026-11-04). The pages' column is 6 inches (576 CSS pixels), so an image `images.downsample` and `images.compress` measure at the column's full width can be drawn wider in the PDF than on a page: a copy keeps at least 88.6 pixels an inch for each time the limit allows, not 96. LuaTeX writes a JPEG into the PDF as its file is (`/DCTDecode`), and a PNG with an alpha channel as two Flate streams, its color and its alpha as a soft mask: of that book's 61.3 MB PDF, the 110 JPEGs are 47.9 MB, their files' bytes, and the 118 PNGs, each with an alpha channel, 7.9 MB, where their files are 11.7 (r42's `scripts/pdfparts.py`). The rest, 5.5 MB, is the fonts, the pages' content, and the structure, which is in object streams already, so rewriting the file without loss saves nothing: pikepdf's save generating object streams made it 61.27 MB, and compressing its Flate streams again, 62.36, larger. A smaller PDF is smaller images, which `images.compress` makes before LaTeX sees them.

**LuaTeX takes a JPEG's resolution from its EXIF data before its JFIF header**: a JPEG 576 pixels wide whose header says 144 pixels an inch and whose EXIF data says 200 is 208.14 points wide in an `\hbox`, 2.88 inches, its EXIF data's (TeX Live 2026, 2026-11-04, r42's `t/exif`); so a copy of a JPEG scaled down, as `images.downsample` and `images.compress` make, has the resolution scaled in both, or it's drawn smaller than the original, as 88 of *Principles of Marketing*'s images were at `small` before it was.

**A drawing's page included in a tagged PDF passes PDF/UA-2, tagged or not.** A drawing that a book with `\DocumentMetadata` renders is a tagged page, its marked content kept (three `BDC` with an `MCID` each for a TikZ circle and its node); `pdfseparate` keeps the page as LaTeX wrote it, `/MarkInfo` marked and no structure tree, and the page included with `\includegraphics[alt=...]` in a tagged document passed veraPDF's PDF/UA-2 profile. So did two of GIAM's drawings as pdfLaTeX made them with its preamble, untagged, its Type 1 fonts embedded and mapped to Unicode (2026-10-30).

**The alt key is made plain with `\pdf_purify:nN`, which leaves the commands Pandoc's writer escapes as their names.** The graphic module's `alt` key runs `\pdf_purify:nN` (read in the graphic module 0.80i), which applies the purify equivalents and the `pdf/purify/setup` hook, hyperref's `\pdfstringdefPreHook` among it, and then `\text_purify:n` (read in pdfmanagement 0.97c). It turns `\&` and `\%` into their characters but has no equivalent for `\textquotesingle`, `\textbar`, `\textless`, `\textgreater`, `\textasciitilde`, or `\textbackslash`, which Pandoc's writer writes into an alt key, so the `/Alt` held their names ("E0\textquotesingle s lowest levels", 27 of _Instrumental Analysis_'s 500 figures; 96 of the 224 alt texts in _Prealgebra 2e_'s third chapter), takes `\^{}` for an accent, and keeps TeX's ligatures (`` ` ``, `''`, `--`, `---`) as typed, since only a font makes them characters (measured with LaTeX 2026-06-01, 2026-10-24 and 25). `latexbuild.PDF_MACROS` declares the six commands' characters as purify equivalents, wherever LaTeX makes text plain, and, for the alt key alone, makes `\^{}` a caret and the ligatures their characters before the graphic module reads the key: a wrapper around `\KV@Gin@alt`, set at `begindocument/before`, when graphicx has defined it, with `\regex_replace_all:nnN` for `\^{}`, since `\tl_replace_all:Nnn` can't match a brace group. A backquote, which the writer writes as itself, is a left quote there, as TeX sets it in the text.

### Tables

**Header rows come from a longtable's head, and header columns only from a setting.** The table module's `tbl/longtable/finalize` plug sets a longtable's header rows from its first head (`\endfirsthead`) or its head (`\endhead`); under PDF 2.0 it tags the repeated head's rows as artifacts, and under PDF 1.x takes them out of the table's children (read in the table module 0.85t). Header columns come only from `\tagpdfsetup{table/header-columns=…}` before the table, and latex-lab's table tagging supports header rows and columns alone, so a table whose cells need a `Headers` list can't be tagged fully. Measured: a longtable's caption is tagged as a first row of one header cell spanning the table, and an empty copy of the repeated head stays inside the table ([latex3/tagging-project#1583](https://github.com/latex3/tagging-project/issues/1583)); NVDA in Acrobat read the caption as row 1, and counted four rows and six columns in a three-by-three table. `pdf-target.lua` declares a table's header column around it, and `lib/pdfretag.py` makes such a cell the table's Caption and takes the empty head out after LaTeX (`pdf.repair_captions`).

**LaTeX's tagging takes a longtable's head for header cells even in a division of paragraphs.** `table/tagging=div` clears the header rows and calls `\__tag_tbl_enable:`, which assigns the `tbl/longtable/finalize` plug a table gets, and that plug sets the header rows again from the longtable's head (`\g__tbl_LT@firsthead_rows_seq` or `\g__tbl_LT@head_rows_seq`) and retags their cells `table/headercell`: each a TH under a NonStruct in a Div, outside any TR. Read in `latex-lab-testphase-table.sty` of 2026-04-28 (0.85t, TeX Live 2026) and in the LaTeX team's development version (`latex-lab-table.dtx`, 2026-09-24, 0.85v, fetched 2026-10-16), which has it too; measured on the Word corpus (veraPDF's PDF/UA-2 profile: ISO 32005's "Sect shall not contain TH", with tagpdf 1.0h) and with a longtable of two rows, the first its `\endhead` (Debian's TeX Live 2026, tagpdf 1.0c: the two TH, not role mapped to a standard namespace, fail 8.2.4). Pandoc's writer puts a table's head in the longtable's head, so `figures-and-tables.lua` writes a layout table's head as rows like the others; a LaTeX book's own longtable with a head, declared `layout`, is tagged with the TH still. A longtable's caption is in its head too, which the division setting tagged a TH with no row, 10 of them in _Prealgebra 2e_'s third chapter, whose numbered step tables have captions ("Table 3.2"); and Pandoc's writer holds the id of a table without a caption in an empty one, in that head (`Writers/LaTeX/Table.hs`, 3.12), so with the caption moved and the id left on the table, the chapter still had its 10 TH with no row, and LaTeX's "Table 3" above each numbered step table, under its own number. So the PDF target tags a layout table `table/tagging=div`, in a group, behind `\ifdefined\tagpdfsetup`, a data table inside it as a table again (tagging on, and the two plugs the division setting changes set back to a table's), with its caption a paragraph before it, which LaTeX doesn't number, and its id an anchor there. _Elementary Algebra 2e_'s step tables, classed `unnumbered`, get no empty caption from the writer, and no `\label` either, so their ids had no anchor in the PDF before that. As PDFs (TeX Live 2026, no contents, 2026-10-19), _Elementary Algebra 2e_'s fifth chapter (11 pages, 5.2 among them, which had stopped LaTeX) is 1,226 Divs and no Table, and has 51 more named destinations than without the anchors, one for each step table; _Prealgebra 2e_'s third, with its numbered step tables, has its 16 data tables as Tables and every TH in a row, each numbered step table's number a paragraph before it and no number of LaTeX's beside it. A candidate for latex3/tagging-project, not reported upstream as of 2026-11-11: under the division setting the finalize plug shouldn't set header rows, or the setting should give longtable one that doesn't. Not found by a web search; the tracker's own search wasn't tried.

**`table/tagging=div` and `presentation` both tag a table's code, under other names.** Each calls `\__tag_tbl_enable:`, assigns `LayoutTable` plugs to `tbl/hmode/begin` and `tbl/vmode/begin`, and clears the header rows and columns; `div` gives the table, its rows, and its cells the `layouttable` structure names, which make it a division of paragraphs, a row's cells in turn, where `presentation` keeps Table, TR, and TD and adds the role presentation (read in the table module 0.85t). PDF/UA-2 says a role shall not contradict its structure element's meaning (ISO 14289-2, 8.2.6.4, as `docs/formats.md` reads it), which a Table "presentation" arguably does, so the PDF target and a LaTeX book's tagged copy tag a layout table and a box with `div`, in a group, since `\tagpdfsetup` holds until it's changed, and only where `\tagpdfsetup` is defined, since an untagged build stops on it. A data table inside such a division is a table again only with `table/tagging=on` and the two plugs set back to `Table` (`texremediate.DATA_OPEN`), which `table/tagging=on` alone doesn't do; veraPDF passes both, and the next table is a table.

**The tagging code doesn't follow `\multirow`, and a longtable doesn't clear its record of a span's cells.** Untold, the tagging code tags a cell spanning rows as its first row's alone and gives each row it covers an empty cell of its own; `table/multirow`, said inside the cell, gives it its RowSpan and records the cells it covers as untagged (`\g__tbl_untagged_cells_prop`). The kernel's `\tbl_init_cell_data_for_table:` clears that record through the `tbl/init/celldata` socket, and array's `\@array` calls it where longtable's `\LT@array` doesn't (read in `array.sty` 2.7b, `longtable.sty` 4.27, and `latex.ltx` 2026-06-01), so every longtable after one with spans lost its cells at the same places (measured). `pdf-target.lua` writes `table/multirow` in each such cell, and `PDF_MACROS` clears the record at `env/longtable/begin`. The tagging status list rates multirow currently incompatible.

**LaTeX's tagging reads a formula whole before setting it, so an `&` in it can end a table's cell.** In a table's cell TeX took a matrix's or an array's `&` for the end of the cell ("Forbidden control sequence found while scanning use of `\__math_grab_inline_delim:w`"), and in a caption for the table's while nameref read its title (_Elementary Algebra 2e_'s 5.2, 2026-10-15). `pdf-target.lua` sets such a formula in braces, and it's a Formula with its MathML file in its TD or its Caption.

**A block quotation in a table's cell isn't allowed in a PDF's structure.** veraPDF reports a BlockQuote in a TD (ISO/TS 32005's TD-BlockQuote), and LaTeX's tagging tags a quotation in a cell as one; `pdf-target.lua` unwraps a quotation in a cell to what it holds.

**Two of PAC's errors on a tagged table are PAC's.** "Table header cell has no associated subcells": latex-lab sets `Scope` through an attribute class, and PAC doesn't resolve `/ClassMap` references, so the same value as an inline `/A` dictionary passes; the maintainers keep the class, which makes `TH-both` expressible for a cell heading both a row and a column ([tagging-project discussion #930](https://github.com/latex3/tagging-project/discussions/930)). "Invalid use of a TR structure element": PAC rejects `Artifact` as a child of `Table`, which is how a repeated longtable head is represented, and which ISO 32000-2's Annex L permits ([issue #1583](https://github.com/latex3/tagging-project/issues/1583)); it's reported to PAC, with no public ticket. Each was verified by building the diagnostic and watching the error go (`docs/troubleshooting.md`).

### Math

**luamml makes MathML only beside unicode-math or lua-unicode-math, unless it's forced.** With neither loaded, the math module warns "Neither unicode-math nor lua-unicode-math has been loaded" and loads no luamml, so a tagged LuaLaTeX build has no MathML; `\tagpdfsetup{math/mathml/luamml/load=true}` forces it with TeX's own fonts, and so does `math/setup` naming `mathml-SE`, `mathml-AF`, or `mathml-MS`, each of which sets luamml to load (read in the math module 0.7e): the calculus notes' own PDF, with `math/setup=mathml-SE` and no unicode-math, has MathML. With TeX's own fonts a symbol TeX builds from pieces comes out as the pieces (`\implies` "=" and "⇒", `\iff` two arrows, `\neq` "=" and U+0338, `\cong` and `\notin` nothing, `\longrightarrow` a minus and an arrow), dots as three periods, and capital Greek dropped, since luamml's OT1 table is commented out (read in `luamml-legacy-mappings.lua`, 0.9.2) and latex-lab registers only two of its three legacy tables, OML and OMS; luamml's own fixes for the arrows (`luamml-patches-kernel.sty`) are switched off under tagging. Forced on GIAM, 536 of 4,521 formulas differed from unicode-math's (2026-10-06). So a LaTeX book's tagged copy loads unicode-math, inside `\ifdefined\directlua` so pdfLaTeX builds as before (`texremediate.MATH_FONTS`), unless the book sets its fonts or math symbols with a package (`texremediate.OWN_FONTS`), whose formulas then get no MathML. An upstream candidate for luamml: OML slot 0x3F, cmmi's `\star`, maps to U+226D, not U+22C6 (read in `luamml-legacy-mappings.lua`).

**luamml makes a box in a formula an `mtext` of what it shows.** `to_unicode` (`luamml-lr.lua`, 0.9.2) takes a glyph as its character, a glue wider than 1000 sp as a no-break space, an hlist's contents in turn, a vlist as U+FFFD, and a rule wider than nothing as U+FFFD, and passes over the rest, an empty hlist among them, as `\hphantom` leaves (read). So `\text{~\hphantom{~~}or~\hphantom{~~}}` is `<mtext> or </mtext>`, a no-break space either side, and `\text{~\hphantom{~~~~~}}` an `mtext` of one (the MathML luamml wrote for a probe book of the PDF suite's two such formulas, 2026-11-04), where `\text{~~~or~~~}` was `<mtext>   or   </mtext>`; `\text{\hphantom{~~~~}}` is an empty `<mtext/>`, and `\textbf{~\hphantom{~~}or~\hphantom{~~}}` `<mtext> or </mtext>`, without its bold (the MathML of the PDF suite's drawn blanks' book, 2026-11-04). `pdf-target.lua` wrote a run of no-break spaces in text in a formula so, for the page's text, until `PDF_MACROS` gave the run one character as LaTeX sets the formula, the formula as written (Artifacts, below). ## fancyvrb and verbatim

**fancyvrb sets `\Verb`'s text in an `\mbox`, so inline highlighted code can't break.** `\FVC@Verb` ends with `\mbox{##2}` (read in fancyvrb 4.6a, 2026/04/16), so Pandoc's inline code with a class, `\VERB`, which the highlighting macros define as fancyvrb's `\Verb[commandchars=\\\{\}]`, can't break anywhere, where plain inline code, `\texttt` with each space a `\ `, breaks at its spaces: a CSV file's text in _Introduction to Python Programming_'s 14.3 ran 627 points past the text, 555 of them off the paper, and its Table 8.2, whose cells hold such code, was too wide for the paper even at half its type (measured 2026-10-28). fvextra 1.14.0's `\Verb` breaks with `breaklines` (`\FV@InsertBreaks` where fancyvrb has the `\mbox`, read in `fvextra.sty`, 2026/02/25), but the tagging status list rates fvextra currently incompatible, and loading it would change fancyvrb under the fitting and wrapping macros. `latexbuild.CODE_SPACES` gives `\VERB`, where it's the highlighting macros' own, fancyvrb's `formatcom*` key (which adds to `formatcom`) with `\OERInlineCodeBox`, which sets `\OERInlineCodeAttribute` to 1 inside `\Verb`'s group, so the code's nodes carry it and the box's first glyph marks the box; `FIT_MACROS`' `oer.inline.code`, the first of its `pre_linebreak_filter` functions, takes such a box apart in its paragraph and adds places to break, after a run of spaces and, in a word, after a run of marks, as a wrapped line of a code block has them (below, Line breaking and fitting). 14.3's span sets in three lines inside the text, and veraPDF passes. `\texttt`, which breaks only at its `\ `, is marked too: `pdf-target.lua` puts each span of inline code in a group with `\OERInlineCode`, which sets the attribute to 2 on what the span holds, and `oer.inline.code` adds the same places to break in a run of glyphs that carries it, where a box whose first glyph carries 1, `\OERInlineCodeBox`'s in `\VERB`'s `formatcom*`, is the one it takes apart.

**Code in struck text is in an `\mbox`, which isn't the code's to take apart.** Inside a soul command, as Pandoc's writer sets struck text (`\st`, which lua-ul gives LuaLaTeX), the writer wraps inline code in `\mbox` (PANDOC-NOTES, the LaTeX writer). So the span's mark is another value, 2, from the box's, 1, and only a box whose first glyph carries 1 is taken apart: struck code keeps its strike, in no language and highlighted, as the PDF suite's code book checks.

**A `\newcommand` with no arguments isn't `\long`, so `\ifx` against a copy made with `\long\def` is false.** `\@yargd@f` puts `\l@ngrel@x`, which `\newcommand` makes `\long`, before the definition only where the command takes arguments (`\ifnum#1>\z@ \l@ngrel@x \fi`, read in `latex.ltx`, 2026-06-01): `\newcommand{\x}{x}` means `macro:->x` and `\newcommand{\y}[1]{x#1}` `\long macro:#1->x#1` (measured 2026-10-28). So a test of whether Pandoc's `\VERB` is the highlighting macros' own, `\newcommand{\VERB}{\Verb[commandchars=\\\{\}]}`, compares it with a copy made with `\def`, as `CODE_SPACES` does; made with `\long\def`, the copy never matched, and the fix did nothing.

**fancyvrb's space beside a color change is no character under LuaLaTeX.** fancyvrb makes a space interword glue (`\FV@Space` as `\ `), and Pandoc colors highlighted code's tokens with `\textcolor`, so in a tagged PDF's text a space beside a color's change was no character at all ("return12-2* p >0"), and a run of spaces, an indentation, was one. A space as `\nobreak\ `, as LaTeX's own verbatim makes one, is a character each, and nothing on the page moves. `latexbuild.CODE_SPACES` sets it (`\fvset{formatcom*={\def\FV@Space{\nobreak\ }}}`), read after Pandoc's highlighting macros, in a book's preamble and a deck's template.

**fancyvrb sets each line in a box with room to spare, so a line too long for its line goes unreported.** A highlighted line runs into the margin with no overfull box, where a line of LaTeX's verbatim, a paragraph, is reported as a line of text too wide; fancyvrb's own line is the text's width less the left margin, a quotation's right margin in it. `FIT_MACROS` measures fancyvrb's lines by the box its characters are set in, through `\FancyVerbFormatLine`, fancyvrb's command for a line, which it wraps and puts back, and LaTeX's verbatim's lines by the line less the glue at its edges, but not what the output routine sets while a block's group is open across a page's break (LuaTeX's `status.output_active`); `latexbuild.CODE_MARKS` tells code's lines from text's in the log (2026-10-27).

**LaTeX's verbatim and latex-lab's set a line's edges differently.** LaTeX's own verbatim sets only a left skip, and its right skip doesn't stretch; latex-lab's sets a paragraph shape, 30 points shorter than a quotation's line in a quotation (as of LaTeX 2026-06-01), and its right skip is the paragraph's, which ragged right gives a stretch, so TeX broke a line of code too long for its line after a hyphen, as LuaTeX may break after one (measured, 2026-10-27 and 28). `FIT_MACROS` keeps a line in no language from breaking after a hyphen (`\exhyphenpenalty`), and ends a wrapped line's parts at the line it measured the block against, whatever the paragraph's own. LaTeX's tagging tags no paragraph in a box holding a wrapped line of fancyvrb's: the line's text is its line's (`block/verbatim/codeline`).

**fvextra marks a line it breaks with a tiny ↪ at the start of each later part.** Its `breaksymbolleft` is `\tiny\ensuremath{\hookrightarrow}` by default (read in fvextra 1.14.0), a math character, so it's text in the PDF, which a copy holds unless something marks it otherwise. `FIT_MACROS` draws its own mark instead, a hook down and to the right (↳) in the two characters a later part is set further in than the line's indentation, as a PDF literal of two strokes in a box of no width: a drawing, which no copy holds, and an artifact under tagging (Artifacts, above).

**fvextra sets a line it breaks in a box, which can't break across pages.** fvextra 1.14.0 puts a broken line's parts in a `\parbox[t]` (read); `FIT_MACROS` sets a wrapped highlighted line the same way, with a strut in each of fancyvrb's lines so the line after a wrapped one is at the block's spacing, so a highlighted line taller than a page runs off its foot, which the build warns of; a line in no language breaks across pages, as a paragraph's lines do (2026-10-28).

**A code block's line is a Sub in PDF 2.0's namespace, and a Span in the RoleMap for a reader of PDF 1.7.** latex-lab's namespace maps the block, `verbatim`, to PDF 1.7's Code, and each of its lines, `codeline`, to PDF 2.0's Sub (`tagpdf-ns-latex-lab.def`; latex-lab-namespace's changes: "changed mapping of verbatim to Code as Sub is now allowed inside Code", 2025/01/12), as LaTeX's verbatim, fancyvrb's environments (latex-lab-testphase-firstaid), and listings tag them, alltt too (`\legacyverbatimsetup`). ISO 32000-2 makes Sub for "a line of source code" (14.8.4.6, Table 367). tagpdf also writes a RoleMap for a reader that doesn't read PDF 2.0's namespaces, a tag mapped there to what its own target maps to in PDF 1.7 (`\__tag_role_add_tag:nn` copies the target's entry in `\g__tag_role_rolemap_prop`), and Sub has none but Span, so `codeline` was a Span: read in tagpdf 1.0i (2026-10-29), in both TinyTeX trees. A block copied from Acrobat was one line, which fits a reader of that RoleMap, and Acrobat does read it: with `codeline` a P there, copying and pasting a sample's blocks from Acrobat gave each line apart, and so did a sample whose block was a Div in that RoleMap too, not a Code, which so isn't needed (Rob, 2026-11-11). Neither kept a line's indentation, which the code's spaces hold as characters (`latexbuild.CODE_SPACES`, a space a `\nobreak\ ` each) and which `pdftotext`, pypdf, and the structure's text have (the PDF suite's code book, the decks suite's `pdftext.code_lines`). Not looked into: whether Acrobat drops leading spaces at a paragraph's start when it copies, which a P would have it do, and so what might keep them (a no-break space Acrobat keeps, ActualText on the line, another role than P); the sample built before the change, a block one line, can't say, its lines run together. A wrapped line's copy, whole or not, wasn't reported. The RoleMap is written by `\__tag_tree_write_rolemap:`, in `\__tag_finish_structure:` after its public hook `tagpdf/finish/before`, where `latexbuild.CODE_LINES` makes `codeline` a P, only where tagpdf has it a Span. veraPDF's PDF/UA-2, PDF/A-4f, and WTPDF profiles pass a PDF either way (measured).

**A `verbatim` right before a fancyvrb `Verbatim` stops LaTeX's tagging.** With no paragraph between them, `\begin{verbatim}…\end{verbatim}\begin{Verbatim}…\end{Verbatim}` in an article under `\DocumentMetadata{tagging=on}` stops at `\end{document}`: "Package tagpdf Error: The number of automatic begin (2) and end (1) text-block para hooks differ!", with LaTeX 2026-06-01 (latex-lab's block code 1.0c) and 2026-11-01 (1.0v), a LaTeX book's tagged copy too; a paragraph between them, or either alone, builds (measured 2026-11-11). Pandoc's writer sets highlighted code in `Shaded` around `Highlighting`, and a Markdown book's code blocks in a row build. An upstream candidate (latex3/tagging-project), not reported as of 2026-11-11.

**listings can't build `\lstinline` in braces whose code holds braces.** `\lstinline{\begin{rouge}x\end{rouge}}`, inline code in braces holding braces, doesn't build with pdfLaTeX and listings, and Pandoc's LaTeX reader reads it to the first `}` (found in review, 2026-11-07; PANDOC-NOTES, the LaTeX reader).

## longtable and booktabs

**longtable stops on a page of two columns.** "longtable not in 1-column mode" (`\if@twocolumn`, read in `longtable.sty` 4.27, 2026-01-28); measured with the class options `twocolumn` and `fleqn` in a book's metadata file and a page with one table (2026-10-21), and a book in two columns built where its table was of Pandoc's class `float`, a `tabular` in a `table` float (2026-10-22). The fitting leaves a floating table as it is, since it measures a table by the widths longtable records, which in a floating table's group were the last longtable's.

**A longtable can't hold another.** A longtable in a longtable's cell stops LaTeX ("Extra alignment tab has been changed to \cr"; GIAM's long division, PANDOC-NOTES, jgm/pandoc#3586). `pdf-target.lua` writes an inner table as a `tabular`, its head declared for tagging in a group of its own, which tagging makes a Table in the cell's TD, veraPDF passing (LaTeX 2026-06-01), or, where a cell spans rows, as lines of text, since multirow isn't loaded there.

**A longtable's rows end in its `\everycr`, and a row's end has hooks.** longtable sets `\everycr` to a `\noalign` that ends the row's tagging (the `tbl/row/end` socket) and runs the kernel's `tbl/row/end` hook, then, while the chunk it sets the table in has room (`\LTchunksize`, 200 rows), counts the next row on and runs `tbl/row/init`; array's `\everycr` does the same for a tabular (longtable 4.27, LaTeX 2026-06-01; read again in `longtable.sty`). So a rule a row asks for can be put after that `\noalign` from either hook (`\group_insert_after:N`), where a rule between rows goes, without touching the writer's `\\`. But at a chunk's end the row is ended by `\LT@echunk`'s `\crcr`, in the chunk, where `\LTchunksize` rows have been counted, and no row is counted on: a booktabs rule put there from `tbl/row/end` stopped LaTeX's tagging at the next row ("Undefined control sequence" on `\ERRORtbl/row`), since latex-lab's firstaid module counts a rule's row off (`\tbl_gdecr_row_count:`, in its versions of booktabs's `\@cmidrulea` and `\@cmidruleb`) for longtable to count on again after it. From `tbl/row/init`, which runs for the next chunk's first row, after that chunk's preamble, the rule comes in the next chunk, as a `\midrule` written after a chunk's last `\\` does (measured with a plain longtable, `\midrule` after its 200th row, which builds). `latexbuild.py`'s row rules (`\OERRowRules`, `\OERRowRule`) use `tbl/row/init`.

**booktabs reads a `(` after a rule as the columns to trim it to, and draws a rule in a longtable as a row of its own.** `\@BLTrule` looks for a `(` (read in booktabs v1.61803398), so a bare `\midrule` before a row opening with "(" would take the row's first characters for that; Pandoc's writer follows each rule with `\noalign{}`, and so does a rule `PDF_MACROS` puts between rows (measured 2026-10-27, a step that opens with "(a)"). In a longtable such a rule is a row of its own across the table's columns, not the line, kept on the page of the row above it, and latex-lab's firstaid module marks it an artifact. A `\midrule` is `\lightrulewidth` thick, .05em (0.6 points in 12-point type), with `\aboverulesep` (.4ex) above and `\belowrulesep` (.65ex) below, and a rule's color comes from `\CT@arc@`, booktabs's hook for colortbl (read). `\OERRowRules` draws OpenStax's gray rule under each row with `\specialrule` at a `\midrule`'s width and spaces, its writer's `\toprule`, `\midrule`, and `\bottomrule` defined to nothing in the table's group.

**longtable sets a table's columns by the widths it recorded on the run before, and the first run's can be wrong.** It records them in `\LT@save@row`, written to the `.aux`; where a table's head is one row, a cell across columns, the first run gives the last of those columns the cell's whole width: _Introductory Business Statistics 2e_'s F tables, "Degrees of freedom in the numerator" across ten columns, Table A1's last column 621 points, the tables 816 points too wide on the first run and 254 on the second (measured 2026-10-21). `FIT_MACROS` measures a table by those widths, fits it on the second run, and fits nothing after it, so that the last run has the pages of the run before, whose labels it reads: fitted on the third run as well, _College Algebra 2e_'s first chapter ended its fourth with "Label(s) may have changed".

**longtable makes `\nopagebreak` a `\noalign`, and the caption package's `\captionsetup` in a longtable is one too.** longtable defines `\nopagebreak` as `\noalign{…}` while it sets a table (read in longtable 4.27), so a `\nopagebreak` in a cell stopped LaTeX, as did a `\captionsetup` in a cell ("Misplaced \noalign": a figure in a table's cell whose caption has its own label, _Instrumental Analysis_'s 26.1). `pdf-target.lua` keeps a heading in a cell with what follows by `\nobreak`, and leaves a figure or a table in a cell, which is no float, as it is.

**A longtable whose `\LTcaptype` is empty sets its caption with no label and no number.** With `\LTcaptype` empty, longtable's `\LT@c@ption` calls `\LT@makecaption` with `\@gobble` and no label (read in longtable 4.27). Pandoc writes an unnumbered table's caption in a group setting `\LTcaptype` to `none`, and the caption package, which Pandoc's template loads for a book with tables, stopped looking for that list (`\ext@none`: "No float type 'none' defined", PANDOC-NOTES). `PDF_MACROS` defines `\ext@none` as a list nothing opens and gives captions of type `none` no label (`\captionsetup[none]`), and `\OERNoCaptionLabel` sets `\LTcaptype` empty where the caption package isn't loaded, as in a deck's PDF, where ltx-talk's label is "Table:".

**LaTeX reports a table too wide once for each row it sets, and a longtable's head twice.** The head is set once for the first page and once for the rest, so one table counts several times among a build's lines too wide: the PDF suite's notes book's table of numbers, a head and a row, three (measured 2026-10-18; `latexbuild.overfull_lines` reads the reports, Line breaking and fitting, below).

## LuaTeX

**LaTeX runs `post_linebreak_filter`'s and `post_mlist_to_hlist_filter`'s functions last registered first.** `ltluatex.lua` declares both `reverselist` and runs such a list from its end (`reverselist_handler`), where `pre_linebreak_filter`, `hpack_filter`, and the other filters of nodes are `list`s, run first registered first, and `add_to_callback` appends a function to the list (first read in TinyTeX's LaTeX, 2026-10-23, and read again in TeX Live 2026's `ltluatex.lua`). So a function registered later sees a paragraph's lines before one registered earlier changes them. `PDF_MACROS` writes `FIT_MACROS` after the blank code, so the fit's measure of a cell's lines runs before `oer.blanks` draws a blank shorter, and the measure counts a blank as three underscores at most.

**`luatexbase.declare_callback_rule` orders a callback's list, and a reverselist runs that list from its end.** On one, as `post_linebreak_filter` and `post_mlist_to_hlist_filter` are, "X before Y" runs Y first (measured on `post_linebreak_filter`, 2026-10-23). The sort a rule sets off moves a function registered after the two it names, a package's among them (luaotfload's color handler for a colored font, babel's `Babel.reset_lines` with `bidi=basic` loaded after the macros, as a deck's header-includes can load it), read in `ltluatex.lua` and measured by a review, so where the order the code is written in will do, it's the safer way.

**A book's PDF runs these on a paragraph before TeX breaks it, in this order:** babel's `Babel.pre_otfload_v`, luaotfload's `luaotfload.node_processor`, tagpdf's `markspaces`, `PDF_MACROS`' `oer.blanks.lines`, `FIT_MACROS`' `oer.inline.code`, `oer.fit.steps`, and `oer.fit.wrap`, and babel's `Babel.unset_atdir` (`pre_linebreak_filter`, as the log's "Inserting" and "Removing" lines leave it in a book of the PDF target's, LaTeX 2026-06-01, 2026-10-28): babel takes luaotfload's and tagpdf's functions out when it loads and puts them back after its own. So the fit measures a worked example's steps after inline code is taken out of its box, its words then the pieces TeX can't break, and tagpdf marks a paragraph's interword spaces before the box is taken apart.

**A function in LuaTeX's `stop_run` callback takes the place of LuaTeX's own last lines.** With one added through `luatexbase` (a list, `ltluatex.lua`), the log ends without LuaTeX's statistics and "Output written on deck.pdf (13 pages, …)", which a deck's build reads for its count of pages: the slides suite's deck read "through ltx-talk: 0 page(s)" where `main` read 13 (measured 2026-11-04, with the first version of `PDF_MACROS`' report of kept blanks). A report at the end goes after the last page instead, from LaTeX's hook `enddocument/afterlastpage`, as `LINE_LENGTH`'s and `PDF_MACROS`' do, the state it reports kept in a global Lua table (`oerline`, `oerblanks`).

**TeX reads Lua in `\directlua` before Lua does.** A percent sign, a hash, a tilde, or a backslash is TeX's, and a Lua comment runs on to the end, TeX joining the lines; `PDF_MACROS`' and `FIT_MACROS`' Lua is written without any of them.

**`node.has_attribute` reads a nil attribute as attribute 0, and luaotfload sets attribute 0 to 0.** `luaotfload-init.lua` sets `tex.attribute[0] = 0` ("The font loader requires that the attribute with index zero be zero", read), so every node then has it: 81 of 81 glyphs on a tagged test page (measured). A measure that read an attribute the macros hadn't made yet, `nil`, took every run of underscores for a blank; `FIT_MACROS` tests that `OERBlankAttribute` exists before reading it (2026-10-23).

**A PDF literal of mode 0 is drawn from where it stands.** A `pdf_literal` whatsit whose `mode` is 0 (origin) is written with the coordinates moved to the current point, so its numbers are relative to where it falls in its line: the arrow before a wrapped line's later part, drawn so from a box at the part's start, came out where its numbers put it, 1.6 to 0.45 of a character before the part's first glyph (measured with pikepdf, 2026-10-29). Its numbers are big points, 65,781.76 scaled points each, written with `string.format`'s `%.3f`, whose percent sign `FIT_MACROS` writes as `string.char(37)` (above).

**LuaTeX strokes a rule thinner than a big point, and draws one whose height is below the baseline.** A rule thinner than a big point is written as a line stroked as wide as it's thick (`0.658 w 0 0 m 20.228 0 l S`, after a `cm` to its middle), a thicker one as a filled rectangle; a rule of negative height, its depth the larger, is drawn from its depth up to its height, both below the baseline: an underscore's ink in IBM Plex Serif at 12 points, height −1.22 points and depth 1.88, was stroked 1.55 below it, 0.658 thick (read with pikepdf, 2026-11-04; the rule's width, height, and depth as `PDF_MACROS` sets them). Renderers draw a stroke or a fill that thin as whole pixels, where a font's rasterizer shades a glyph's edges: at 96 and 150 pixels an inch, poppler drew such a rule as one black row and the underscore glyph beside it as two gray ones, and Ghostscript the rule darker than the glyph, so a glyph left beside a rule looks like a dash of its own (measured, 2026-11-04; why `PDF_MACROS` draws a blank wholly as a rule).

**TeX can't read a width past `\maxdimen`, where Lua can.** A highlighted line longer than about 2,275 characters at 12 points stopped LaTeX ("Dimension too large", at `\box_wd:N`); `FIT_MACROS` measures such a line, and a paragraph it's about to break, in Lua, by what it holds, whose sums run past TeX's largest dimension (2026-10-28).

**TeX drops a math node at a line's start, as it drops glue after a break.** (TeXbook, chapter 14.) So a line that opens in a formula broken before it has the formula's closing node and not its opening one, and a measure that took the formula's spaces for the spaces between words measured such a line at a tenth of its column (2026-10-22). In a book's PDF, a line a formula breaks ends with a node closing a text direction after the penalty, before the right skip, which babel's `bidi=basic` puts there.

**microtype's protrusion kern isn't always beside its character.** microtype puts a `margin_kern` node after the character a line ends with, but after the penalty a paragraph's last line ends with, where that's last, and before the `local_par` node that opens a paragraph's first line (22 of 24 right kerns and 12 of 36 left ones for an underscore, in _Writing Guide with Handbook_'s 10.5); the node's `glyph` field is a copy without the character's attributes (measured, 2026-10-23). And LuaTeX hangs only a character, nothing beside a strut, while a table's cell opens its first line and ends its last with the array package's strut, a rule, so a cell's first line hangs nothing at its start, nor its last at its end. `PDF_MACROS`' blank code finds a kern's character past the nodes of no width between them.

**A break's penalty gives its attributes to the line's right skip, and tagpdf writes a space at a line's end as a character.** In a paragraph `FIT_MACROS` breaks, a penalty after a run of spaces, with the attributes of the character before the space, keeps the line's text as written, the space at the line's end; with the space's own attributes, tagpdf wrote a second space at each break, and a break before the space lost it (shown on a paragraph of its own, 2026-10-28).

**A discretionary break's text after it opens the next row, so a hang can be the break's own.** A `disc` node's `pre` list ends the row a break there ends, its `post` list opens the next row, and its `replace` list is set where TeX doesn't break; LuaTeX takes the node's `penalty` field for the break's, TeX weighs such a break as a hyphen's (TeXbook, chapter 14), and one of subtype 0 is a break TeX considers on its first pass. TeX discards glue and penalties at a row's start after a break, which an empty `hlist` there stops; a glue node TeX breaks at becomes the row's right skip, its attributes with it, and a right skip TeX makes takes the attributes of the node it breaks at (measured building the hang, 2026-10-29). `oer.inline.code`'s `hang_lines` makes each place a paragraph of code line by line may break a discretionary whose `post` is a box as wide as the hang, the arrow at its end, so that TeX measures each row with it and breaks the paragraph once; a paragraph shape made from a first breaking moved the breaks of the ragged paragraph. After a run of spaces, which the row keeps at its end, the discretionary's `pre` is a kern of minus their width, so the row isn't counted wider for them, which had moved a row's last word to the next (*Introduction to Python Programming*'s 1.9); and the discretionary takes the attributes of the line's first glyph, since with the spaces' it gave the row's right skip the attribute tagpdf writes a space for, and the structure's text had two at each break.

**luaotfload's HarfBuzz mode sets a right-to-left mark before its letter, where node mode sets it after.** In a font loaded `mode=harf`, as the fallback fonts are (`fonts.hashes.identifiers[font].hb`), a Hebrew point is set before the letter it's on, so a break between them left the point on the line before (both modes shown on a Hebrew word in DejaVu Sans, 2026-10-28). Where a mark has no width, TeX takes the later of two breaks it sits between, which keeps it with its letter anyway.

**LuaTeX has neither pdfTeX's commands nor `\pdfoutput`, and luatex85 gives it both.** An old test, `\ifx\pdfoutput\undefined`, takes LuaLaTeX for LaTeX with dvips, as GIAM's workbook and solutions manual choose their class options; rewriting the test sent LuaLaTeX into the pdfTeX branch, whose commands (`\ifnum\pdfoutput>0`, `\pdfinfo`, `\pdfcatalog`) it lacks. ## Line breaking and fitting

**TeX names a line it can't fit by where its box came from, and by the file it's reading.** Its report of an overfull box (TeXbook, chapter 14: `\hbadness`, `\hfuzz`) says "in paragraph at lines", "in alignment at lines" for a table's row, or "detected at line" for a box made whole, a display formula. In the contents or a list of figures or tables, the line is the list's own file's (`book.toc`); a footnote's code is the file `footnote-code.lua` wrote it to, and a footnote's own paragraphs the line its text ends on. `PDF_MACROS` wraps `\@starttoc`, which the classes' lists go through, to mark in the log where each list is set, and `latexbuild.overfull_lines` reads the reports from Pandoc's `--log`. It's wrapped as it's defined: the kernel's and most classes' take one argument, the AMS classes' two, the list's heading after its file's extension (`amsbook.cls`, line 1022, `amsart.cls`, line 1147; a wrapper of one had taken its own `\typeout` for the heading), as `\cs_parameter_spec:N` gives them, `#1` or `#1#2`. A document command has no parameters of its own, so its spec is empty: one of mandatory arguments alone is `\__cmd_start_optimized:` and its code, a command named with a space, `\@starttoc code`, whose spec is its arguments' (`\RenewDocumentCommand\@starttoc{m}`, skrapport's, is `\__cmd_start_optimized: \@starttoc code`, the code `#1`); one of two mandatory arguments `\protect\bar` and a space, a robust command's form; one with an optional argument `\__cmd_start:nNNnnn {om}…`, with the specification written in it (measured with `\cs_replacement_spec:N`, LaTeX 2026-06-01, 2026-11-03). The first is wrapped as its code takes its arguments; the others, and anything defined some other way, are left alone. `\GetDocumentCommandArgSpec` would say, but it's xparse's, the package's, not the kernel's (read in `xparse.sty`).

**TeX breaks a formula in text only after a relation or a binary operator outside braces.** (TeXbook, chapter 18: `\relpenalty` and `\binoppenalty`.) A script is part of its atom, so `Ag^+` doesn't break; `\left(` makes a group TeX sets apart; and an environment's lines (`gathered`, `aligned`) don't break at all. `pdf-target.lua` sets both penalties to 10000 in a worked example's steps' group, so a formula there is fitted, not broken (2026-10-22).

**A run of underscores can't break.** A question ending in a fill-in blank too long for what's left of its line ran into the margin (24.3 points, _Introduction to Sociology 3e_'s chapter 19 quiz), and Pandoc's template's `\emergencystretch` of 3em can't make room on a line of few spaces. `PDF_MACROS` draws a run of four or more underscores that `pdf-target.lua` marks (`\OERBlank`) shorter where it runs past its line, once LaTeX has broken the paragraph (2026-10-19), and draws every blank in a tagged PDF as a rule at shipout (Artifacts, above), where a face has the underscore's measure (Fonts, below); one without it keeps its underscores, which the build warns of, from a line `PDF_MACROS` writes to the log after the last page (`enddocument/afterlastpage`, LuaTeX, below).

**In ragged lines a line holding only a label costs TeX nothing.** Its badness is 0 (`\tracingparagraphs`), so TeX broke the line after a label and set its long blank on the next. In text set flush to both margins, such a line, or one ending at a blank drawn at two thirds of its line, is too loose to take, and TeX keeps a label with a blank too long for its line itself, taking the line too wide as a last resort. With no way to break a line without an overfull box, TeX takes the last break it was still considering (artificial demerits), which split a label of two words from its blank where a no-break space joined them to it. `\linebreak[1]` is a penalty of −51 (`\@lowpenalty`, 51 in the standard classes, read in `book.cls`). `PDF_MACROS` draws a long blank at two thirds of its line while TeX breaks a ragged paragraph and asks for a break after it with that penalty (2026-10-24).

**`\emergencystretch` gives TeX a third pass, which decides where a justified paragraph breaks when no break is within `\tolerance`.** Pandoc's template sets it to 3em, so TeX gives each line that much more stretch and takes a line looser than `\tolerance` allows rather than one too wide: a label and its blank were a line of their own, underfull (badness 1184), where without it TeX's last resort was a line 3.64 points too wide, broken at a hyphen in the next label (measured in a LaTeX file of its own, 2026-10-24; PANDOC-NOTES, the LaTeX writer).

**In a ragged paragraph every line costs TeX the same, so a break's penalty decides; set flush to both margins, a loose line weighs against it.** A line's demerits are (`\linepenalty` + its badness) squared plus its break's penalty squared, `\linepenalty` 10 (TeXbook, chapter 14), and with a right skip whose stretch is infinite, as the PDF's ragged right is (`\@flushglue`), every line's badness is 0: a break at a penalty of 120 costs 14,400 more than a break at a space, more than 140 more lines would, so TeX takes it only where no break at a space fits, in a word longer than its line. Set flush to both margins, a line's badness counts too, and a penalty of 120 is cheaper than a line of badness above 110, a little looser than the font's stretch allows. Measured on a page of inline code (Pandoc 3.13, LaTeX 2026-06-01, 2026-10-28), a word of code of 40 characters after six or seven words, too long for what their line had left, in twelve placements: ragged, TeX put it whole on the next line in each, whichever of three rules the breaks inside a word had. Flush to both margins, with a break allowed between any two characters, at 120 after a mark and 150 between letters, TeX broke the word where it filled the line, in most placements between letters ("att" over "ribute_name"); with breaks only in a word longer than the line, it took lines too wide as its last resort, up to 102 points past the margin, the line before the word too short to stretch within `\tolerance` even with the template's `\emergencystretch`; with breaks after a run of marks in any word and between letters only in one longer than its line, it broke after a mark ("(arg)." over "attribute_name"), and no line ran over but one made of code alone, whose spaces don't stretch, 5.4 points too wide. `FIT_MACROS`' `oer.inline.code` has the last rule, a break between letters or inside a run of marks at 200, and a wrapped line of a code block the same penalties, its right skip stretching as far as the line is wide. At 150, one break between letters cost less than two after runs of marks (22,500 against 28,800), and TeX parted a path's two backslashes, or a folder's name, to save a row in four of the PDF suite's twelve paths of different lengths; at 200 it doesn't.

**`\raggedright`'s `\\` ends the paragraph, and LaTeX sets footnotes, captions, and minipages justified again.** `\raggedright` sets `\@rightskip` to `\@flushglue` and makes `\\` `\@centercr`, which ends the paragraph with `\par`, and `\@parboxrestore`, which a footnote, a float's caption, and a minipage each begin with, sets `\rightskip` and `\@rightskip` to nothing (`\@arrayparboxrestore`; read in `latex.ltx`, 2026-06-01). **A paragraph's full lines can be counted as TeX sets them, after it breaks the paragraph.** `latexbuild.LINE_LENGTH`, for the page meant to meet WCAG 1.4.8 (`wcag-aaa: true`), counts in `post_linebreak_filter` each line of a paragraph but its last, set at the text's width (`tex.hsize` as it is when the document begins), with no paragraph shape or hanging indentation, outside the output routine, whose glyphs are nine tenths or more in the text's face at the document's start (`font.current()`, ligatures counted by what they're made of): its glyphs and its interword glue, a space a character, as W3C's technique counts them (C20), and LaTeX writes the count to the terminal and the log at the end. **A paragraph break in a box's words, and in a quotation's** (TeX Live 2026, LuaLaTeX, measured 2026-11-07). A box sets its words on one line: `\mbox{un.\par deux.}` is "un.deux.", the spaces after `\par` skipped, and `\mbox{un. \par deux.}` "un. deux."; `\hbox` and ulem's `\uline` with a blank line are "un. deux.", the first line's end a space and the blank line's `\par` nothing; `\underline`, `\uline`, and `\sout` with `\par` are "un.deux.". csquotes' `\enquote{un.\par deux.}` sets two paragraphs with one quotation over both, « un. ¶ deux. ». `\emph`'s, `\textbf`'s, `\href`'s, and `\foreignlanguage*`'s words can't hold one ("Paragraph ended before … was complete"), where `\foreignlanguage`'s can.

**babel takes the first `main=` it's given, and a second is an error** (pdfLaTeX, babel 26.13, measured 2026-11-07): `\PassOptionsToPackage{main=french}{babel}` before `\usepackage[main=english,french]{babel}` is an error, "Bad option 'main=english'. Either you have misspelled the key or there is a previous setting of 'main'", past which French is the main language, the passed option first in babel's list; so is `[main=french,english,main=english]`; a `\PassOptionsToPackage` after the package is loaded is ignored; and `\RequirePackage[main=french,english]{babel}` is read as `\usepackage` is. A `main=` with a modifier (`ngerman.foo`) is kept whole, and babel loads no language for it.

**Which commands' text takes a paragraph break, or a list** (TeX Live 2026, measured 2026-11-08, pdfLaTeX unless said). The font commands' (`\textbf`, `\emph`, and their kin, hyphenat's `\textnhtt`) stop on a paragraph break ("Paragraph ended before \text@command was complete"), as do `\textsuperscript`'s and `\textsubscript`'s, `\href`'s ("… before \href@split …"), xcolor's `\textcolor`'s ("… before \@textcolor …"), and polyglossia's `\textfrench`'s (LuaLaTeX: "… before \textfrench …", defined `O{} m`); polyglossia's `\textlang`'s text takes one (`O{} m +m`), and so do `\NoCaseChange`'s and hyphenat's `\nohyphens`'s. soul's `\ul`, `\st`, `\hl`, `\so`, and `\caps` set a blank line, or a space and `\par`, as two paragraphs with the command's look on each, but `\par` right after a word with no space runs that word into the next paragraph's first ("un.deux."), soul's scanner taking `\par` before it has set the word. A list in their text: `\textbf`'s, `\textfrench`'s, and `\textcolor`'s take one, set between the words before it, in their paragraph, and the words after it, colored for `\textcolor`; soul's stop ("Environment itemize undefined"), and `\colorbox`'s ("Not allowed in LR mode"). `\vbox{un.\par deux.}` and a `\vbox` holding a list build, a box of paragraphs the line's width, its last line on the line it's in; `\hbox to 3cm{…}`, `spread`, and `\vbox to` build. url's `\path|…|` and `\path{…}` set their text as code, and `\url|…%|` at the top level sets its `%` (in another command's argument, a `%` is a comment, as TeX reads that argument before `\url` can take it as it's written).

**csquotes' marks** (csquotes 5.2p, LuaLaTeX, babel 26.13, measured 2026-11-08). By default, every quotation has the style of the main language, whichever language it's in, csquotes loaded before babel or after: in a French book, `\enquote{un \enquote{deux} trois}` is « un “deux” trois », `\enquote*{x}` “x”, and `\foreignquote{english}{y}` and `\hyphenquote{english}{x}` « y » and « x ». With `autostyle`, each has the style of the language it's in: `\foreignlanguage{ngerman}{\enquote{w \enquote{v}}}` „w ‚v‘“, `otherlanguage`'s and a `\selectlanguage`'s language too, `\foreignquote{english}{y}` “y” in its own, but `\hyphenquote{english}{x}` « x » in the one around it. French's « » have a no-break space inside, 0.8 of a word space (2.65 points against an interword space's 3.32 at 10pt), which babel-french's `\mkfrenchopenquote` sets and its own Lua code writes as U+00A0. csquotes' styles for each language are in `csquotes.def`, by babel's names and their aliases (`ngerman` is `german`'s „ “ ‚ ‘; `british`'s are ‘ ’ “ ”), and a language's variant its options give is that variant's style: with `german=guillemets`, a German book's `\enquote{a \enquote{b} c}` is »a ›b‹ c«, with `english=british` an English one's ‘a “b” c’, and with `french=guillemets*` a French one's inner quotation « » too; `\setquotestyle[guillemets]{german}` in the preamble sets »a ›b‹ c« whatever the main language. csquotes reads its options in order (`\ProcessOptions*`), those `\PassOptionsToPackage` gives it before it's loaded first; one given after it's loaded changes nothing. `style=` turns `autostyle` off: with `[english]{babel}`, `[style=german]` and `[style=german,autostyle=false]` set „a ‚b‘ c“, but `[style=german,autostyle=once]`, or `tryonce`, the default, “a ‘b’ c”, the main language's style, taken at the preamble's end. A `\setquotestyle` in the preamble turns `autostyle` off, and `\setquotestyle*` turns it on: with `[french,english]{babel}`, `\enquote{a} \foreignlanguage{french}{\enquote{b}}` is then “a” « b ». Without babel or polyglossia, `autostyle` is off. Latin's style is an alias of the variant it takes (`latin/italianguillemets`, Italian's « » by default), so under `autostyle` `\foreignlanguage{latin}{\enquote{x}}` is «x». A main language csquotes has no style for (`\babelprovide[import,main]{esperanto}`) is set in its fallback style, a bold ? for each mark, with the warning `csquotes.sty` gives as "No style for language '…'. Using fallback style".

**babel's `\babelprovide[main]`** (babel 26.13, read in `babel.sty` and measured 2026-11-08 with LuaLaTeX). `\babelprovide` with `main` among its options makes its language babel's main one as the line is read (`\main@language`, for a language it makes and one it renews alike), in place of the one babel's options gave: with `\usepackage[english]{babel}`, `\babelprovide[import,main]{french}` makes French the main language, and csquotes' default style French's, `\enquote{a \enquote{b} c}` « a “b” c »; Latin made main so, «a “b” c»; and with `[english,provide=*]{babel}`, `\babelprovide[import,main]{ngerman}` „a ‚b‘ c“.

**babel's `main=` in a file the preamble reads** (pdfLaTeX, babel 26.13, measured 2026-11-08): `\PassOptionsToPackage{main=ngerman.foo}{babel}` in a file the preamble `\input`-s, and `\RequirePackage[ngerman,english]{babel}` in a package of the book's own loaded after it, set English as the main language, saying nothing, as the same lines in the master do.

**babel takes another of its names for a language, or the name in capitals, for the language** (LuaLaTeX, babel 26.13, TeX Live 2026, measured 2026-11-11). With `\usepackage[french,english]{babel}`, `\foreignlanguage{francais}{…}` sets its words with French's hyphenation (`\language` 6, `\languagename` `francais`), and `\foreignlanguage{French}{…}` as French (`\languagename` `french`), neither with a warning: babel looks a name up as written, then in lowercase, then in capitals (`\bbl@fixname`, read in `babel.sty`), and `francais` is a synonym of `french` in TeX's `language.dat`. Given as an option of babel's, `francais` stops LaTeX ("Unknown option 'francais'"). **kpathsea finds a file whose name differs from the one asked for only in case, on Linux too, while `texmf_casefold_search` is on.** Measured 2026-11-11 with LuaLaTeX from TeX Live 2026 (LuaHBTeX 1.24.0, kpathsea 6.4.2), where `kpsewhich -var-value=texmf_casefold_search` gives 1: `\includegraphics{GRE_intro.pdf}` and `\includegraphics{GRE_intro}` both included `gre_intro.pdf` from the document's folder, and `kpsewhich GRE_intro.pdf` gave `./gre_intro.pdf`; with `texmf_casefold_search=0` in the environment the same file stopped at "File `GRE_intro.pdf' not found". ## Other programs in a PDF's build

What the programs that read, check, or rewrite a PDF do. pypdf's and pikepdf's versions matter because Ubuntu 24.04's packages (pypdf 4.0.2 and pikepdf 8.7.1, under Python 3.12) are older than pip's.

### pypdf

**pypdf before 4.1 doesn't follow a reference read through what `.get()` returns.** With Ubuntu 24.04's pypdf 4.0.2, `latexbuild.unembedded_fonts` failed on every figure Ghostscript rewrote, so a LaTeX book's figures kept unembedded fonts and failed PDF/UA; seven checks failed and the PDF suite stopped, which Python 3.12, pypdf 4.0.2, and pikepdf 8.7.1, Ubuntu 24.04's apt packages, reproduce exactly, and pip's newer ones had hidden (2026-10-13). And the decks suite's `pdf_sizes` called `.get` on a page's resources, an indirect object, which pypdf 5 reads as what it names and 4.0.2 doesn't (AttributeError; 2026-10-19). Each helper reads what an indirect object names first.

**pypdf finds an untagged PDF's spaces by the gaps between its runs, and its versions don't agree on them.** pypdf before 5.1 infers a space where the font changes, which three of the decks suite's checks had matched too literally (2026-10-13). 4.0.2, Ubuntu 24.04's, reads a justified line's wider spaces as two, where 6.21.2 passed the same check (2026-10-27); reads an untagged table's row, inside a layout table, as "x5y6" (2026-10-15); leaves out a space that 5.9.0 reads after a term's note marks in an untagged copy, "Both19,20Its" for "Both19,20 Its", the marks drawn as they should be (2026-11-10); and on a small LuaLaTeX PDF (`\textsubscript`, `\textsuperscript`, and math) reads "H 2O", "CO 2", and "E=mc2", where 5.9 and 6.21.2 read each line as it's printed, "H2O", "CO2", and "E = mc2" (2026-10-23). From 6.4.1, pypdf knows the standard fonts' widths (`CORE_FONT_METRICS`; before, it guessed every glyph at twice a space's width), and it reads a gap of half a space between two runs as a space, where 4.0.2 and 5.9 saw no gap: a typesetter's subscript touches its letter, but one in a PDF written by hand in Helvetica without its widths read "H 2O". And 4.0.2, 5.9.0, and 6.21.2 alike run an untagged PDF's words together where it's set in IBM Plex, where `pdftotext`'s and MuPDF's text doesn't: such a PDF draws no spaces, and pypdf finds them by the gaps (2026-10-27). So the suites' checks of pypdf's text take any run of spaces, or none, where the versions differ, with a gap that matters measured from the glyphs with pikepdf, and the PDF suite's books whose checks were measured in Latin Modern's widths name its faces (`LATIN_MODERN`: set in Plex, 36 of their checks failed, each on a measure Plex's wider letters change or on pypdf's text; with pypdf 4.0.2, three of the untagged book's checks of its text failed so too). `pdfcheck`'s reading of a line passes a line's text on as pypdf gives it.

**pypdf's text visitor gives some runs no place around marked content.** Around marked content, as a fill-in blank's Span is, a run's place is the origin, or a transformed page's: in 4.0.2 the runs after it, in 5.9 those before (measured on a LuaLaTeX build, 2026-10-22 and 23). So `pdfcheck`, finding the line of text a box is on for the output check's `--verbose`, takes a run's place from its first show operator in `visitor_operand_before`; a run shown with `'` or `"` takes its place after the move to the next line (`visitor_operand_after`), where pypdf 4.0.2 places it, 5.9 placing it lower, by its leading times the type's size; a page pypdf reads partway is read as nothing; and spaces pypdf puts before a mark, and between a blank's overlapping underscores where one was drawn shorter to fit its line ("_ _ _ _"), come out. The line is the one whose baseline the run the box starts in is on, with what's raised or lowered on it less than 0.4 of the box's height off, so a subscript nearer the box's foot isn't taken for it: at 12 points, LuaLaTeX lowers a subscript 2.96 points and raises a footnote's mark about 4.3 (measured in review).

**pypdf reads a URI without a UTF-16 byte-order mark as PDFDocEncoding.** A URI is UTF-8 in PDF 2.0 and ASCII in 1.7, so `pdfcheck` decodes a link's address from `original_bytes` (a `bytearray` in 4.0.2, `bytes` in 5.9), and reads one kept in an object of its own through it (2026-10-22 and 23).

### pikepdf

**pikepdf 10 warns, on saving, of a form field's annotation that no form reaches.** The decks suite's fixture for the PDF tidy had one, and pikepdf's warning (`_count_orphaned_widgets`) showed among the suite's lines; the field is in `/AcroForm` now, as hyperref puts one, and the check records warnings and wants none (2026-10-14).

**pikepdf 8.7.1 has no `values()` on a dictionary.** With Ubuntu 24.04's pikepdf 8.7.1, `pdfglyphs.fonts` took the AttributeError for resources pikepdf can't follow and returned nothing to mend, so the unit suite's checks found no font mended, Python 3.12's suites alone failing. It reads a dictionary's `items()`, which 8.7.1 and 10.13 both have, and the checks pass with either (2026-10-30).

**Python reuses the `id()` of pikepdf's wrappers.** `pdfglyphs.fonts` told direct dictionaries apart by the `id()` of pikepdf's wrappers; a wrapper was freed and its `id()` given to another's, so a form's or a page's fonts after the first with resources of their own were skipped: GIAM's drawings' pages hold 93 such forms (found in review, 2026-10-30). A direct dictionary has one place in the file, so it's reached once and isn't told apart at all now.

### latexmk

**A `.latexmkrc` that sets `$out_dir` moves the PDF from where the run looks.** `-outdir=. -auxdir=.` on the command line wins, and `convert.py` runs latexmk with both (2026-10-06).

### Ghostscript

**Ghostscript doesn't embed the base-14 fonts even with `-dEmbedAllFonts=true`.** They're on its `NeverEmbed` list; `-c "<</NeverEmbed [ ]>> setdistillerparams"` empties it, and the URW clones go in as subsets. `pdftocairo -pdf` embeds too, at five times the time and twice the size, so it's the fallback; both were checked with veraPDF on a figure in a tagged document (2026-10-07). veraPDF had failed _OpenIntro Statistics_ on 8.4.5.5.1 (361 checks, fonts not embedded) and 8.4.5.8 (73, ZapfDingbats glyphs without Unicode), all in R's figures: 548 of the 563 PDFs draw with Helvetica, ZapfDingbats, or Symbol unembedded. `latexbuild.embed_figure_fonts` does it for PDFs of at most three pages, four at a time: 546 in 26 seconds.

**Ghostscript's automatic filter can make a Flate raster a JPEG.** Rewriting a figure to embed its fonts, pdfwrite chose JPEG for an image the figure held Flate-compressed (found in review, 2026-10-07). `latexbuild`'s Ghostscript command turns the automatic filter off and names Flate (`-dAutoFilterColorImages=false`, `-dColorImageFilter=/FlateEncode`, and the same for gray).
